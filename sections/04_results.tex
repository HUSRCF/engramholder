\subsection{Do Native--Rotated score differences vary between heads?}
\label{sec:interaction_result}
\paragraph{Prospective tests of fixed models.}
For Protenix, we selected the Train384/ESMC recipe from observed evidence
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:recipe_selection}),
then evaluated all 24 fixed adapters and Query on Fresh192 without further
training. The sole primary interaction is
$\Psi=\result{p192_pair_interaction}$
[\result{p192_pair_interactionLo}, \result{p192_pair_interactionHi}].
Factor's Native--Rotated score difference is
\result{p192_pair_factor_rotation}, while G+'s is
\result{p192_pair_gplus_rotation}; its interval includes zero without
establishing equivalent Native and Rotated G+ scores. Fresh192 establishes
a larger signed score difference for Factor than for G+ under this fixed
recipe on screened short-chain targets (Appendix~\ref{app:fresh192});
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} retains all five means.

For OpenFold, we evaluated the 24 fixed Train96/ESM2 adapters and Query on
Fresh96, locked before prediction, without further training
(Appendix~\ref{app:fresh96}). Factor's score difference remains positive:
\result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotation}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationHi}],
but the sole primary interaction is \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}].
It is not established on new targets; a positive Factor difference cannot
replace this endpoint. The studies differ in predictor, training scale,
features and panel, so their significance outcomes do not estimate a
between-predictor difference in $\Psi$.

\paragraph{Additional training and inference seeds.}
On the now-observed panels, three new training seeds per predictor give
$\Psi=\rebuttalProtenixNewPsi$ for Protenix and
$\rebuttalOpenFoldNewPsi$ for OpenFold (conditional 95\% target intervals).
All three Protenix training-seed estimates are positive; its evaluation
uses three paired inference seeds. The cohorts are reported separately
from the original fits. Exploratory training-seed/target resampling also
retains a positive Protenix interval, but does not estimate uncertainty
for new random rotations (Appendix~\ref{app:followup_seeds}).

\paragraph{Observed-panel comparisons.}
The OpenFold Train96/ESM2 interaction is positive on Confirm96-B and
Length48; Protenix Train96 also supports it on Confirm96-B
(Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:interaction_scope}).
These observed-panel results do not replace new-target tests.
Seed/rotation marginals and supplementary metrics are reported in
Appendices~\ref{app:complete_results} and \ref{app:dh_fourcells}.
Under the OpenFold Train96/ESM2 recipe, signed channel permutations retain
a positive Confirm96-B interaction, while the Length48 interval includes
zero. The effect is therefore not confined to dense mixing, although ideal
AdamW coordinate compatibility does not establish equivalent FP32 trajectories
(Appendix~\ref{app:signed_control}).

\subsection{What changes with training budget and injection schedule?}
\label{sec:budget_schedule}
\paragraph{A fixed budget extension.}
After Fresh192 was observed, we continued all 24 Protenix/ESMC models from
1536 to 3072 updates with their AdamW states and unchanged learning rate.
All four cells improve (Table~\ref{tab:budget_schedule_overview}), and
Factor's Native--Rotated score difference decreases.
The prespecified final interaction remains positive:
$\Psi_{3072}=\result{budget_interaction_3072}$
[\result{budget_interaction_3072Lo}, \result{budget_interaction_3072Hi}].
G+'s difference becomes more negative in point estimate, but neither its
paired change nor the paired change in $\Psi$ is established. This extends
beyond the original stopping point without establishing convergence,
budget invariance or another new-target confirmation
(Appendix~\ref{app:budget_extension}).

\paragraph{Injection schedule and absolute quality.}
A separate OpenFold Train96 study retrains First-only, All and Last-only
injection with gradients across four passes and a common Train/Dev-calibrated
learning rate. Computational-budget constraints limited the amended scope
to R1 and 36 fits; this amendment preceded unified scoring
(Appendix~\ref{app:schedule_retraining}). All yields higher mean Factor
scores than First-only, but a smaller interaction:
$\Psi_{\mathrm{All}}-\Psi_{\mathrm{First}}=
\result{schedule_D_write_R1}$
[\result{schedule_D_write_R1Lo}, \result{schedule_D_write_R1Hi}].
Among the three tested schedules, First-only has the largest estimated
interaction and the lowest mean pair-lDDT in each of the four cells
(Table~\ref{tab:budget_schedule_overview}). Under All, the interaction
interval lies above zero, whereas Factor's difference interval includes
zero and G+'s lies below zero. A larger interaction need not identify a
better schedule; a positive
interaction need not establish a positive Factor difference. Jointly
retraining each schedule does not isolate a feedback mechanism.

\subsection{Where do effects transfer, and do they imply a better head?}
\label{sec:esmc}
\label{sec:protenix}
\label{sec:length}
Protenix Factor retains Native--Rotated advantages across the tested
residual forms, checkpoints, mean-preserving rotations and Length48
(Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:scope}). At Train384, interactions are positive
with both ESM2 and ESMC on observed panels; OpenFold establishes them with
neither feature source. ESMC improves absolute quality in both predictors
without establishing changes in $\Delta_F$ or $\Psi$. Feature family,
width, precision and projection size change together
(Appendix~\ref{app:dh_fourcells}).

convergence nor a uniform learning delay

\subsection{When does adaptation help with MSA inputs?}
\label{sec:msa_utility}
We trained Native Factor and G+ at matched MSA settings and compared them
with the unadapted backbone (Table~\ref{tab:msa_utility}). On observed
Fresh192, Factor outscored both G+ and Mini-default at 32- and 256-row
caps, but did not establish a gain at 512 rows or full MSA. At 512 rows,
G+ outscored Factor. The full-MSA baseline remained stronger than the
capped adapters. These comparisons change training and evaluation depth
together; artificial caps do not identify a universal depth threshold
(Appendix~\ref{app:followup_msa}).

On observed Fresh96, OpenFold Factor's 32-row gain has a conditional
interval just above zero, but its exploratory training-seed/target
interval includes zero: $\rebuttalOpenFoldMsaSeedInterval$.
Factor's advantage over G+ is not established. This is limited evidence
of adaptation utility, not a replication of the rotation interaction.

We also evaluated the fixed Protenix MSA adapters on a retrospective
CASP16 subset. Among eight eligible A1 targets without component suffixes,
the 32-row Factor gain passes the two-comparison correction; the 256-row
gain does not. G+ also benefits at 32 rows, and neither cap establishes
Factor superiority over G+. Full-MSA Mini-default has the highest mean
score. The small, length-restricted subset does not establish blind CASP
generalization (Appendix~\ref{app:casp16}). These results support evaluating
heads at the intended MSA depth by absolute validation quality.

\subsection{Do mechanism diagnostics explain the interaction?}
\label{sec:e2_compensation}
Jointly training each OpenFold Factor head with its own identity-initialized,
mean-preserving orthogonal map improves the rotated arms in both executions,
but preferential benefit over Native is not re-established in the same-seed
repeat (Appendix~\ref{app:e2_compensation}). The map is shared across targets,
pairs and recycles within each model. A separate Protenix frozen-head map
study improves both Native and Rotated arms; its preferential gain interval
crosses zero when training seeds are resampled
(Appendix~\ref{app:followup_map}). Reconstruction error also fails to
predict post-training direction gaps across eight rotations
($\rho=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_rho}$,
exact one-sided $p=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_p}$;
Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}).

A new test uses shared-head gradients at initialization to predict
favorable and unfavorable rotations before full training. Eight pairs
matched in rotation strength yield 96 fits and three paired inference
seeds on observed Fresh192. The predicted Factor good-minus-bad contrast
is $\rebuttalRotationFactor$; its difference from G+'s contrast is
$T=\rebuttalRotationT$. Both intervals resample rotation pairs and targets;
neither meets the prespecified positive prediction
(Appendix~\ref{app:rotation_prediction}). The tested initialization-based
criterion therefore does not explain the final structure-score differences.

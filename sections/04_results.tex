\subsection{What changes with training budget and injection schedule?}
\label{sec:budget_schedule}

1536 to 3072 updates with their AdamW states and unchanged learning rate.
All four cells improve (Table~\ref{tab:budget_schedule_overview}). Factor's
Native--Rotated score difference falls from \result{budget_factor_rotation_1536} to
\result{budget_factor_rotation_3072}, a paired change of
\result{budget_factor_rotation_change}
[\result{budget_factor_rotation_changeLo}, \result{budget_factor_rotation_changeHi}].

$\Psi_{3072}=\result{budget_interaction_3072}$
[\result{budget_interaction_3072Lo}, \result{budget_interaction_3072Hi}].
G+'s signed score difference becomes more negative in point estimate, from
\result{budget_gplus_rotation_1536} to \result{budget_gplus_rotation_3072};

\subsection{Does shared output freedom preferentially help rotated Factor?}
\label{sec:e2_compensation}
The four-cell interaction compares complete parameterizations and does not
isolate output freedom. To test whether adding an output map gives rotated
Factor a larger gain than Native Factor, we jointly train its parameters $\theta$ and a shared
mean-preserving orthogonal map $C$:
\begin{equation}
 U_N=U_0+C\Delta U_\theta,\qquad U_R=U_0+CR\Delta U_\theta.
 \label{eq:compensated_injection}
\end{equation}
This study uses two mean-preserving rotations from the separate eight-rotation
prediction study, selected by low/high reconstruction error before their
task-training scores were observed. They differ from the preceding four-cell
study's three dense rotations. One 8,001-parameter $C$ is shared across targets,
pairs and recycles within each model. Both $R$ and $C$ belong to
$\mathcal H=\{Q\in SO(128):Qu=u\}$, where $u$ is the normalized all-ones vector.
For this subgroup the compensated Factor function classes are equal in ideal
arithmetic: $C_R=C_NR^\top$ absorbs $R$ (Appendix~\ref{app:e2_compensation}).
Each execution trains nine compensated models from $C=I$ and zero residual
under the fixed OpenFold/Train96/ESM2/1536 recipe. This is joint retraining,
with no inverse-rotation initialization.

Let $B_N=S(N+C)-S(N)$ and $B_R^{C}=S(R+C)-S(R)$, averaging $B_R^{C}$ over
both rotations. We call $T_C=B_R^{C}-B_N$ the additional compensation gain
for rotated Factor, relative to Native Factor's gain. It equals the reduction
in the Native--Rotated score difference after adding $C$. Unlike $\Psi$,
which compares Factor with G+, $T_C$ compares gains within Factor, not the
absolute scores of the compensated models.

Table~\ref{tab:e2_absolute} shows positive rotated-arm gains in both executions:
\result{e2_pair_B_R} and \result{e2rep_pair_B_R}. The estimated gains for rotated
Factor are similar across executions, whereas Native's estimated gain is larger
in the repeat than in the original execution
(\result{e2rep_pair_native_change} versus \result{e2_pair_native_change}).
Only the original execution establishes a larger compensation gain for rotated
Factor than for Native Factor:
$T_C=\result{e2_pair_T_C}$ versus \result{e2rep_pair_T_C} in the repeat.
Native improves in both, so the original smaller mean gap is not produced by
harming it. The change in interval significance establishes neither a

Descriptively, the original $B_R^C$ is positive on
\result{e2_pair_B_R_count_positive}/96 targets, but $T_C$ on only
\result{e2_pair_T_C_count_positive}/96; only one rotation separately establishes

\paragraph{Same-seed retraining repeat.}
The nine $+C$ fits were repeated with the same seeds and 1536-update recipe,
keeping the original no-$C$ models fixed. This adds no independent seeds and
is not pooled with the original execution. A positive additional compensation
gain for rotated Factor was not re-established. Despite identical starting
states, the numerical training trajectories can differ; the cause of the
full-training outcome difference remains unresolved
(Appendix~\ref{app:e2_repeat}). Output freedom and joint optimization remain coupled.

\paragraph{Predicting post-training score differences.}
A prespecified teacher-update reconstruction diagnostic did not establish
its predicted ranking of Native--Rotated score differences across eight tested
rotations ($\rho=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_rho}$, exact one-sided
$p=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_p}$; Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}). The useful
rotated-arm compensation gains do not validate that prediction or identify
output freedom as the unique explanation of the four-cell interaction.

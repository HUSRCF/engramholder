\subsection{Do Native--Rotated score differences vary between heads?}
\label{sec:interaction_result}
\paragraph{Prospective tests of fixed models.}
For Protenix, we selected the Train384/ESMC recipe from observed evidence,
then evaluated all 24 fixed adapters and Query on Fresh192 without further
training. The sole primary interaction is
$\Psi=\result{p192_pair_interaction}$
[\result{p192_pair_interactionLo}, \result{p192_pair_interactionHi}].
Factor's Native--Rotated score difference is
\result{p192_pair_factor_rotation}, while G+'s is
\result{p192_pair_gplus_rotation}; its interval includes zero without
establishing equivalent Native and Rotated G+ scores. Fresh192 establishes
a larger signed score difference for Factor than for G+ under this fixed
recipe on screened short-chain targets (Appendix~\ref{app:fresh192});
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} retains all five means.

For OpenFold, we evaluated the 24 fixed Train96/ESM2 adapters and Query on
Fresh96, locked before prediction, without further training
(Appendix~\ref{app:fresh96}). Factor's score difference remains positive:
\result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotation}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationHi}],
but the sole primary interaction is \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}].
It is not established on new targets; a positive Factor difference cannot
replace this endpoint. The studies differ in predictor, training scale,
features and panel, so their significance outcomes do not estimate a
between-predictor difference in $\Psi$.

\paragraph{Observed-panel comparisons.}
The OpenFold Train96/ESM2 interaction is positive on Confirm96-B and
Length48; Protenix Train96 also supports it on Confirm96-B
(Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:interaction_scope}).
These observed-panel results do not replace new-target tests.
Seed/rotation marginals and supplementary metrics are reported in
Appendices~\ref{app:complete_results} and \ref{app:dh_fourcells}.
Under the OpenFold Train96/ESM2 recipe, signed channel permutations retain
a positive Confirm96-B interaction, while the Length48 interval includes
zero. The effect is therefore not confined to dense mixing, although ideal
AdamW coordinate compatibility does not establish equivalent FP32 trajectories
(Appendix~\ref{app:signed_control}).

\subsection{What changes with training budget and injection schedule?}
\label{sec:budget_schedule}
\paragraph{A fixed budget extension.}
After Fresh192 was observed, we continued all 24 Protenix/ESMC models from
1536 to 3072 updates with their AdamW states and unchanged learning rate.
All four cells improve (Table~\ref{tab:budget_schedule_overview}). Factor's
Native--Rotated score difference decreases, with a paired change of
\result{budget_factor_rotation_change}
[\result{budget_factor_rotation_changeLo}, \result{budget_factor_rotation_changeHi}].
The prespecified final interaction remains positive:
$\Psi_{3072}=\result{budget_interaction_3072}$
[\result{budget_interaction_3072Lo}, \result{budget_interaction_3072Hi}].
G+'s difference becomes more negative in point estimate, with a paired
change interval including zero. The interaction change is
$K=\result{budget_K}$ [\result{budget_KLo}, \result{budget_KHi}],
which does not establish an overall change. These results extend beyond
the original stopping point without establishing convergence, budget
invariance or another new-target confirmation

\paragraph{Injection schedule and absolute quality.}
A separate OpenFold Train96 study retrains First-only, All and Last-only
injection with gradients across four passes and a common Train/Dev-calibrated
learning rate. Computational-budget constraints limited the amended scope
to R1 and 36 fits; this amendment preceded unified scoring
(Appendix~\ref{app:schedule_retraining}). All yields higher mean Factor
scores than First-only, but a smaller interaction:
$\Psi_{\mathrm{All}}-\Psi_{\mathrm{First}}=
\result{schedule_D_write_R1}$
[\result{schedule_D_write_R1Lo}, \result{schedule_D_write_R1Hi}].
First has the largest interaction and lowest absolute quality
(Table~\ref{tab:budget_schedule_overview}). Under All, Factor's difference
has an interval including zero while G+'s is negative, yielding positive
$\Psi$. A larger interaction therefore need not identify a better schedule
or a positive Factor difference. Jointly retraining each schedule does not
isolate a feedback mechanism.

\subsection{Where do effects transfer, and do they imply a better head?}
\label{sec:esmc}
\label{sec:protenix}
\label{sec:length}
Protenix retains Native--Rotated advantages across the tested residual
forms, checkpoints and mean-preserving rotations, and on complete sequences
in Length48 (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:scope}). Length transfer does not
establish family or pretraining novelty. At Train384, Protenix interactions
are positive with both ESM2 and ESMC on the observed panels, while OpenFold
establishes them with neither feature source
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:interaction_scope}). ESMC improves prediction
quality in both predictors without establishing changes in $\Delta_F$ or
$\Psi$. Feature family, width, precision and projection size change together
(Appendix~\ref{app:dh_fourcells}); model size is not isolated.

\label{sec:limits_results}
Direction effects do not determine head competitiveness. Factor exceeds
G+ in the matched Protenix comparison, whereas G+ wins both OpenFold
long-chain comparisons (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:gplus}). Neither adaptation
benefit nor interaction is established for AtlasFold with either feature
source; high baseline and native AtlasLM remain unisolated explanations
(Appendix~\ref{app:dh_fourcells}). Training-set changes also alter data
composition and repetition counts, preventing a pure data-size attribution
(Appendix~\ref{app:training_psi}). Saved Dev8 checkpoint curves establish
neither convergence nor a uniform learning delay
(Appendix~\ref{app:checkpoint_curves}). Table~\ref{tab:coverage} reports
all four-cell means and backend differences.

\subsection{Does shared output freedom preferentially help rotated Factor?}
\label{sec:e2_compensation}
In the Native and two preselected Rotated conditions, we jointly train
each Factor head with its own mean-preserving orthogonal map $C$.
Initialized at identity, $C$ is shared across targets, pairs and recycles
within that model (Appendix~\ref{app:e2_compensation}). Let
$B_N=S(N+C)-S(N)$ and $B_R^C=S(R+C)-S(R)$, averaging the latter over both
rotations; $T_C=B_R^C-B_N$ compares their compensation gains.
Rotated Factor improves in both the original execution and a complete
same-seed repeat using the same fixed no-$C$ baselines
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:e2_absolute}). Only the original establishes
a larger compensation gain than Native:
$T_C=\result{e2_pair_T_C}$
[\result{e2_pair_T_CLo}, \result{e2_pair_T_CHi}],
versus \result{e2rep_pair_T_C}
[\result{e2rep_pair_T_CLo}, \result{e2rep_pair_T_CHi}] in the repeat.
The repeat adds no independent seeds and is not pooled with the original;
its $T_C$ interval includes zero. Differing interval significance does not
establish a between-execution population difference
(Appendix~\ref{app:e2_repeat}). A prespecified reconstruction diagnostic
also did not establish its predicted ranking of post-training score
differences across eight rotations ($\rho=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_rho}$,
exact one-sided $p=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_p}$;
Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}). The useful rotated-arm gains do not
validate that prediction or identify output freedom as the unique
explanation of the four-cell interaction; output freedom and joint
optimization remain coupled.

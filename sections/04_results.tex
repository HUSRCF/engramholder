\subsection{Rotation sensitivity interacts with adapter parameterization}
\label{sec:interaction_result}
Under the matched OpenFold Train96 recipe, output rotation affects the factor
head more than G+ (Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}). On Confirm96-B, the factor
contrast is \result{inter_c96_factor_rotation}, while G+'s contrast is
\result{inter_c96_gplus_rotation}. The directly estimated interaction is
\result{inter_c96_interaction}
[\result{inter_c96_interactionLo}, \result{inter_c96_interactionHi}].
The Length48 secondary comparison is also positive:
\result{inter_l48_interaction}
[\result{inter_l48_interactionLo}, \result{inter_l48_interactionHi}].
These are differences-in-differences on a 0--1 score scale, not relative
percentage improvements.

The main-metric interaction is positive in all three seed marginals and all
three rotation marginals on both panels, but in 8/9 and 7/9 individual
seed-by-rotation cells, respectively. Target-level interactions are positive
for 61/96 and 34/48 proteins. Supplementary lDDT and TM-score interaction
intervals agree in direction, although a Length48 TM-score seed marginal is
negative. The effect is therefore not confined to a single marginal instance,
and it is not positive for every fitted combination.

G+'s direction intervals include zero on both panels; its Length48 rotated mean
is slightly higher. This does not establish equivalence of trained G+ systems.
Instead, the direct interaction shows different rotation sensitivity under the
tested parameterizations and optimizer. The function-class control rules out a
loss of representable functions as a necessary effect of rotating G+, while
leaving optimization dynamics as part of the empirical comparison.

\subsection{Native orientation benefits factor adaptation in tested Protenix settings}
\label{sec:protenix}
The original Protenix-Mini tangent comparison on Confirm96-B gives a native
advantage of \result{pt_tangent} [\result{pt_tangentLo}, \result{pt_tangentHi}],
with 78/96 positive targets. Full gives \result{pt_full24}
[\result{pt_full24Lo}, \result{pt_full24Hi}]. The tangent construction serves to
isolate first-order updates; it is not a uniformly better adapter than Full.
Using Tiny's own factors and decoder retains an advantage of
\result{pt_tiny} [\result{pt_tinyLo}, \result{pt_tinyHi}], extending the evidence
to a second checkpoint within the same family.

A mean-preserving orthogonal control, $Ru=u$ for the all-ones unit vector $u$,
also underperforms native updates: \result{pt_mean}
[\result{pt_meanLo}, \result{pt_meanHi}]. Preserving this special channel
direction therefore does not remove the effect in the tested setting. It does
not isolate all downstream normalization behavior. These follow-ups, together
with the Full training-scale comparisons, are shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:scope}. They establish a scope for the factor direction effect;
they do not constitute additional factor-versus-G+ rotation interactions.

\subsection{Fixed adapters transfer beyond their adaptation-training lengths}
\label{sec:length}
Without further training, the fixed Train384 Full adapters retain an advantage
on Length48 of \result{pt_length} [\result{pt_lengthLo}, \result{pt_lengthHi}],
with 41/48 positive targets. Native and rotated means are
\result{pt384_l48_native} and \result{pt384_l48_rotated}, both above the
unadapted mean of \result{pt384_l48_query} (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).
Thus the contrast is accompanied by useful adaptation in mean score, rather
than only less damage from one update family. Baseline comparisons here remain
descriptive secondary results. Supplementary residue-average lDDT and TM-score
support the same direction.

This study tests complete sequences beyond the adapters' training-length
range, not arbitrary-length prediction or family novelty. The pretrained
folding model and PLM may already have encountered long or related proteins.
Official Mini-ESM scores \result{pt384_l48_official} on the same panel, leaving
a substantial quality gap. Length transfer strengthens the tested applicability
of the adapter but does not establish a competitive replacement for the full
native system.

\subsection{Native-source updates retain structural advantages at matched global norms}
\label{sec:norm_cross}
On 24 observed Confirm96-B targets, we cross learned residual source and global
Frobenius norm for fixed Protenix Train384/Full/1536 models. Write
$\delta_a=m_ad_a$, where $m_a=\|\delta_a\|_F$, $\|d_a\|_F=1$, and
$a\in\{N,R\}$. Each trained rotation is paired with the same-seed native model.
We inject $m_Nd_N$, $m_Rd_N$, $m_Nd_R$, or $m_Rd_R$ throughout c4/s5 inference,
scaling completed pair residuals rather than factor increments. Norms use no
reference-structure mask. All 720 pure and mixed predictions are rerun on the
same backend; none fails. The mixed residuals are inference-time interventions
and need not correspond to outputs of the trained factor head or states
encountered during training.

Here, \emph{direction source} includes learned content and relative pair/channel
magnitudes after global normalization, unlike the original channel-rotation
control. For score $Y_{ab}$ with direction source $a$ and norm source $b$, the
primary effect is
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm dir}=\tfrac12[(Y_{NN}-Y_{RN})+(Y_{NR}-Y_{RR})].
 \label{eq:norm_source}
\end{equation}
Whole targets are resampled within four fixed length strata, after averaging
paired seeds and rotations; intervals condition on these fitted models.

Native-source updates remain better at both matched norms
(Table~\ref{tab:norm_cross}). The average effect is positive for 18/24 targets
and all three seed and rotation marginals; residue-average lDDT and TM-score
agree in direction. Global norm alone is therefore insufficient to explain
this structural gap. The norm-source effect is \result{cross_ca_Eamp}
[\result{cross_ca_EampLo}, \result{cross_ca_EampHi}], and the interaction is
\result{cross_ca_I} [\result{cross_ca_ILo}, \result{cross_ca_IHi}]; neither
establishes a stable effect. This does not establish norm irrelevance or
additivity (Appendix~\ref{app:norm_cross}).

Earlier Train24/tangent diagnostics found an oracle-space advantage but no
established learned-direction advantage at $10^{-3}\|U_q\|_F$; actual-scale
task response favored Native despite smaller mean norms
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}). The model recipe, preceding state, amplitude and
endpoint differ here. These results therefore do not form a same-model mechanism
chain or identify how training produced the residuals.

\subsection{Competitiveness and limits across predictors}
\label{sec:limits_results}
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} places direction effects next to absolute performance
and the generic comparator. In the matched Protenix Train384 follow-up, Native
exceeds G+ by \result{pt384_c96_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_c96_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_c96_gdiffHi}] on Confirm96-B and
\result{pt384_l48_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_l48_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_l48_gdiffHi}] on Length48.
The differences are positive for 78/96 and 43/48 targets and all three paired
seed means. G+ itself improves over query-only by \result{pt384_c96_gquery}
and \result{pt384_l48_gquery} in mean score. Thus Native's advantage holds
against a functioning explicit-factor generic comparator under this common
learning rate and budget, not against a separately optimized G+ upper bound.
The long-chain comparison combines historical H100 predictions with new MI250
G+ predictions; replay checks are limited engineering evidence, not a
panel-wide numerical error bound (Appendix~\ref{app:engineering}).

OpenFold Train384 gives
\result{of384_c96_direction}
[\result{of384_c96_directionLo}, \result{of384_c96_directionHi}] on Confirm96-B,
without an established advantage. On Length48 it gives
\result{of384_l48_direction}
[\result{of384_l48_directionLo}, \result{of384_l48_directionHi}].
G+ exceeds the native factor head on the two OpenFold long-chain comparisons;
Table~\ref{tab:gplus} gives the paired estimates. A direction benefit within
one constrained family can coexist with a better generic adapter.

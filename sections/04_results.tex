\subsection{An observed-panel interaction is not established on new targets}
\label{sec:interaction_result}
Under the matched OpenFold Train96/ESM2-35M recipe with 1536 AdamW updates, output rotation affects the factor
head more than G+ (Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}). On Confirm96-B, the factor
contrast is \result{inter_c96_factor_rotation}, while G+'s contrast is
\result{inter_c96_gplus_rotation}. The directly estimated interaction is
\result{inter_c96_interaction}
[\result{inter_c96_interactionLo}, \result{inter_c96_interactionHi}].
The Length48 secondary comparison is also positive:
\result{inter_l48_interaction}
[\result{inter_l48_interactionLo}, \result{inter_l48_interactionHi}].
These are differences-in-differences on a 0--1 score scale, not relative
percentage improvements. The completed OpenFold Train384 four-cell controls do not
establish the interaction with either ESM2 or ESMC
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:interaction_scope}); the positive finding is recipe-specific.

On both observed panels, all seed and rotation marginals are positive, but only
8/9 and 7/9 individual seed-by-rotation cells are positive, respectively.
On Confirm96-B, the residue-average lDDT interaction is
\result{inter_c96_interaction_residue}
[\result{inter_c96_interaction_residueLo}, \result{inter_c96_interaction_residueHi}],
and the TM-score interaction is \result{inter_c96_interaction_tm}
[\result{inter_c96_interaction_tmLo}, \result{inter_c96_interaction_tmHi}].
These metrics score the same structures, not independent samples.

G+'s direction intervals include zero on both panels; its Length48 rotated mean
is slightly higher. This does not establish equivalence of trained G+ systems.
Instead, the direct interaction shows different rotation sensitivity under the
tested parameterizations and optimizer. The function-class control rules out a
loss of representable functions as a necessary effect of rotating G+, while
leaving optimization dynamics as part of the empirical comparison.

We then evaluated these same 24 adapters and query-only baseline on Fresh96,
locked before prediction under the sequence-screening protocol in
Appendix~\ref{app:fresh96}. All 2,400 predictions completed successfully, with
no new training. Mean scores for F/RF/G+/RG+/query are
\result{fresh_factor_native}/\result{fresh_factor_rotated}/\result{fresh_generic_plus_native}/\result{fresh_generic_plus_rotated}/\result{fresh_query_native}.
Factor retains a direction effect of \result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotation}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_factor_rotationHi}],
but the sole primary interaction is \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}].
It is not established on new targets; the positive Factor contrast cannot
replace this endpoint. Supplementary interaction intervals also include zero.

\subsection{Native orientation benefits factor adaptation in tested Protenix settings}
\label{sec:protenix}
The original Protenix-Mini tangent comparison on Confirm96-B gives a native
advantage of \result{pt_tangent} [\result{pt_tangentLo}, \result{pt_tangentHi}],
with 78/96 positive targets. Full gives \result{pt_full24}
[\result{pt_full24Lo}, \result{pt_full24Hi}]. The tangent construction isolates first-order updates, not a uniformly better adapter.
Using Tiny's own factors and decoder retains an advantage of
\result{pt_tiny} [\result{pt_tinyLo}, \result{pt_tinyHi}], extending the evidence
to a second checkpoint within the same family.

A mean-preserving orthogonal control, $Ru=u$ for the all-ones unit vector $u$,
also underperforms native updates: \result{pt_mean}
[\result{pt_meanLo}, \result{pt_meanHi}]. Preserving this channel direction does not remove the effect or isolate
all downstream normalization. The original scope comparisons are retained in
Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:scope}.

The completed Train96/Full follow-up adds three G+ and nine rotated-G+ fits,
matching the training/development manifests and 1536-update budget of the
OpenFold comparison, with Protenix's own fixed recipe. On Confirm96-B,
$\Delta_F=\result{p96_pair_factor_rotation}$ and
$\Delta_{G+}=\result{p96_pair_gplus_rotation}$ give
$\Psi=\result{p96_pair_interaction}$
[\result{p96_pair_interactionLo}, \result{p96_pair_interactionHi}].
All three seed and rotation marginals are positive; 61/96 target interactions
are positive. This extends observed-panel interaction support to a second
predictor, without providing a new-target confirmation.

The Train384 feature study completes both ESM2 and ESMC four cells
(Table~\ref{tab:coverage}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:interaction_scope}). Its prespecified
Confirm96-B ESMC Factor contrast is \result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_factor_rotation}
[\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_factor_rotationLo}, \result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_factor_rotationHi}].
The follow-up $\Psi$ is \result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_interaction}
[\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_interactionLo}, \result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_interactionHi}];
Length48 gives \result{dh_protenix_l48_c_pair_interaction}
[\result{dh_protenix_l48_c_pair_interactionLo}, \result{dh_protenix_l48_c_pair_interactionHi}].
ESM2 interactions also have positive intervals. ESMC G+ itself has positive
rotation contrasts (\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_gplus_rotation}/\result{dh_protenix_l48_c_pair_gplus_rotation});
function-class closure does not ensure equivalent finite-budget training.
The direct interactions establish that Factor's differences are larger here.

\subsection{Fixed adapters transfer beyond their adaptation-training lengths}
\label{sec:length}
Without further training, the original ESM2 Train384 Full adapters retain an advantage
on Length48 of \result{pt_length} [\result{pt_lengthLo}, \result{pt_lengthHi}],
with 41/48 positive targets. Native and rotated means are
\result{pt384_l48_native} and \result{pt384_l48_rotated}, both above the
unadapted mean of \result{pt384_l48_query} (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).
Both adapted families improve in mean score; baseline comparisons are
descriptive. Supplementary residue-average lDDT and TM-score are concordant.

This complete-sequence study tests adapter length transfer, not family novelty
or exclusion from backbone/PLM pretraining.
Official Mini-ESM scores \result{pt384_l48_official} on the same panel, leaving
a substantial quality gap rather than establishing a competitive replacement.

\subsection{Feature substitution improves quality without a resolved change in sensitivity}
\label{sec:esmc}
OpenFold's feature study adds 33 fits and 4,752 predictions; the Protenix/AtlasFold
study adds 66 fits and 9,504 predictions. All predictions complete successfully.
Each retains its original final-1536-update rule, common optimizer recipe and
observed panels. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} reports all completed four cells and
baselines; the studies' endpoint identities remain separate.

ESMC raises Protenix Native scores from
\result{dh_protenix_c96_e_native}/\result{dh_protenix_l48_e_native} to
\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_native}/\result{dh_protenix_l48_c_native}
on Confirm96-B/Length48. G+ also improves. OpenFold Native improves by
\result{a_c96_native_gain}/\result{a_l48_native_gain}, and G+ by
\result{a_c96_gplus_gain}/\result{a_l48_gplus_gain}.
Yet direct ESMC--ESM2 changes in $\Delta_F$ and $\Psi$ have intervals including
zero for both predictors. Protenix retains positive interaction intervals;
OpenFold establishes them with neither Train384 feature source. Higher
structural quality therefore need not coincide with an established change in
rotation sensitivity. The substitution changes PLM family, feature width,
cache precision and input-projection size together; it does not isolate model
size or test intermediate-layer or multilayer features.

\label{sec:norm_cross}
A full-inference robustness analysis crosses learned residual source and global
Frobenius norm on 24 observed Confirm96-B targets, using fixed Protenix
Train384/Full/1536 models. All 720 pure and mixed predictions are rerun on the
same backend. Native-source advantages are \result{cross_ca_D_mN} at the
native norm and \result{cross_ca_D_mR} at the rotated norm; the symmetric
source effect is \result{cross_ca_Edir}
[\result{cross_ca_EdirLo}, \result{cross_ca_EdirHi}]. Thus global norm alone is

\subsection{Competitiveness and limits across predictors}
\label{sec:limits_results}
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} places direction effects next to absolute performance
and the generic comparator. In the matched Protenix Train384/ESM2 follow-up, Native
exceeds G+ by \result{pt384_c96_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_c96_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_c96_gdiffHi}] on Confirm96-B and
\result{pt384_l48_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_l48_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_l48_gdiffHi}] on Length48.
The differences are positive for 78/96 and 43/48 targets and all three paired
seed means. G+ itself improves over query-only by \result{pt384_c96_gquery}
and \result{pt384_l48_gquery} in mean score. Thus Native's advantage holds
against a functioning explicit-factor generic comparator under this common
learning rate and budget; neither optimal G+ performance nor an upper bound is established.
For Protenix Train384/ESMC on Confirm96-B, the Native head gap is
$F-G^+=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_native_minus_gplus}$, while
$RF-RG^+=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_rotated_head_gap}$ after rotation.
Their difference is $\Psi=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_interaction}$.
Thus a substantial head gap remains under rotation. This mean identity is
not a causal percentage decomposition or an additional significance test.
The ESM2 long-chain comparison combines historical H100 predictions with new MI250
G+ predictions; replay checks are limited engineering evidence, not a
panel-wide numerical error bound (Appendix~\ref{app:engineering}).

OpenFold Train384 gives
\result{of384_c96_direction}
[\result{of384_c96_directionLo}, \result{of384_c96_directionHi}] on Confirm96-B,
without an established advantage. On Length48 it gives
\result{of384_l48_direction}
[\result{of384_l48_directionLo}, \result{of384_l48_directionHi}].
G+ exceeds the native factor head on the two OpenFold long-chain comparisons;
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:gplus} gives all paired competitiveness estimates. A direction benefit within
one constrained family can coexist with a better generic adapter.

AtlasFold's complete ESM2 and ESMC matrices establish neither useful added
adaptation nor a Factor direction advantage or interaction. Under ESMC,
Confirm96-B $\Delta_F=\result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_factor_rotation}$
[\result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_factor_rotationLo}, \result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_factor_rotationHi}],
with $\Psi=\result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_interaction}$
[\result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_interactionLo}, \result{dh_atlas_c96_c_pair_interactionHi}].
Length48's $\Psi$ interval also includes zero. Retaining the native AtlasLM
while adding ESMC does not establish a remedy for this interface. This boundary
cannot be uniquely attributed to baseline quality or feature redundancy.
Atlas's ESMC third seed runs on H100 and the other seeds on MI250; the
cross-feature comparison for that seed changes backend too. Protenix ESMC's
complete four cells run on MI250, while historical ESM2 Length48 comparisons
retain their mixed-backend limitation (Appendix~\ref{app:dh_fourcells}).

The fixed-budget Factor contrast, $(N-R)_{384}-(N-R)_{96}$, uses Full and
1536 updates throughout. It is
\result{pt_scale} [\result{pt_scaleLo}, \result{pt_scaleHi}] for Protenix on
Confirm96-B, versus \result{of_scale_c96}
[\result{of_scale_c96Lo}, \result{of_scale_c96Hi}] for OpenFold.
The corresponding OpenFold Length48 estimate is
\result{of_scale_l48} [\result{of_scale_l48Lo}, \result{of_scale_l48Hi}].
On Confirm96-B, all four OpenFold/ESM2 configurations improve in mean score
from Train96 to Train384; rotated Factor improves more than native Factor,
reducing their gap rather than causing an absolute performance decline.
These contrasts change training-set composition and repetition at fixed update
count (16 versus 4 average passes). The short-chain gap shrinks in OpenFold but
grows in Protenix; OpenFold Length48 establishes no change. Separately, the
post-hoc paired change $\Psi_{384}-\Psi_{96}$ under ESM2 is
\result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddt}
[\result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddtLo}, \result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddtHi}] on Confirm96-B,
and \result{psi_train_l48_ca_lddt}
[\result{psi_train_l48_ca_lddtLo}, \result{psi_train_l48_ca_lddtHi}] on Length48.
Only the short-panel interaction change is established by these unadjusted
intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:training_psi}). Data coverage, optimization at fixed
updates and training-set composition remain unseparated explanations.

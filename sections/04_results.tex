\subsection{When does native orientation benefit Factor adaptation?}
\label{sec:protenix}
The original Protenix-Mini tangent comparison on Confirm96-B gives a native
advantage of \result{pt_tangent} [\result{pt_tangentLo}, \result{pt_tangentHi}],
with 78/96 positive targets. Full gives \result{pt_full24}
[\result{pt_full24Lo}, \result{pt_full24Hi}]. The tangent construction isolates first-order updates, not a uniformly better adapter.
Using Tiny's own factors and decoder retains an advantage of
\result{pt_tiny} [\result{pt_tinyLo}, \result{pt_tinyHi}], extending the evidence
to a second checkpoint within the same family.

A mean-preserving orthogonal control, $Ru=u$ for the all-ones unit vector $u$,
also underperforms native updates: \result{pt_mean}
[\result{pt_meanLo}, \result{pt_meanHi}]. Preserving this channel direction does not remove the effect or isolate
all downstream normalization. The original scope comparisons are retained in
Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:scope}.

\label{sec:length}
Without further training, the original ESM2 Train384 Full adapters retain an advantage
on Length48 of \result{pt_length} [\result{pt_lengthLo}, \result{pt_lengthHi}],
with 41/48 positive targets. Native and rotated means are
\result{pt384_l48_native} and \result{pt384_l48_rotated}, both above the
unadapted mean of \result{pt384_l48_query} (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).
Both adapted families improve in mean score; baseline comparisons are
descriptive. Supplementary residue-average lDDT and TM-score are concordant.

This complete-sequence study tests adapter length transfer, not family novelty
or exclusion from backbone/PLM pretraining.
Official Mini-ESM scores \result{pt384_l48_official} on the same panel, leaving
a substantial quality gap rather than establishing a competitive replacement.

\subsection{Can targeted output freedom mitigate direction cost?}
\label{sec:e2_compensation}
We intervene within Factor by jointly training its parameters $\theta$ and a
shared mean-preserving orthogonal map $C$:
\begin{equation}
 U_N=U_0+C\Delta U_\theta,\qquad U_R=U_0+CR\Delta U_\theta.
 \label{eq:compensated_injection}
\end{equation}
One 8,001-parameter $C$ is shared across targets, pairs and recycles within each
model. Its ideal function class can absorb the two preselected rotations
(Appendix~\ref{app:e2_compensation}). All nine new fits start at $C=I$ and zero
residual, using the fixed OpenFold/Train96/ESM2/1536 recipe; this is joint
retraining, not inverse-rotating a previously trained model.

Let $B_N=S(N+C)-S(N)$ and $B_R=S(R+C)-S(R)$, with $B_R$ averaged over the two rotations.
The predeclared mean $B_R=\result{e2_pair_B_R}$
[\result{e2_pair_B_RLo}, \result{e2_pair_B_RHi}] exceeds Native's improvement
$B_N=\result{e2_pair_native_change}$ (Table~\ref{tab:e2_absolute}).
The mean reduction in direction cost is
$T_C=B_R-B_N=\result{e2_pair_T_C}$
[\result{e2_pair_T_CLo}, \result{e2_pair_T_CHi}]. Unlike $\Psi$, which compares
Factor with G+, $T_C$ tests the effect of adding $C$ within Factor.
Native also improves, so the smaller mean gap is not produced by harming it.

For effects averaged within each target, descriptively, $B_R>0$ for
\result{e2_pair_B_R_count_positive}/96 targets (median
\result{e2_pair_B_R_median}), whereas $T_C>0$ for
\result{e2_pair_T_C_count_positive}/96 (median \result{e2_pair_T_C_median}).
These post-hoc descriptions add no new endpoint. Only one rotation separately
establishes $T_C>0$; the average TM-score $T_C$ interval includes zero.
The result supports useful average mitigation in this setting, not equivalence
or uniform benefit. It changes output freedom and joint optimization together
and does not validate the failed reconstruction predictor.

\subsection{Does direction sensitivity explain adapter competitiveness?}
\label{sec:esmc}
ESMC raises Protenix Native scores from
\result{dh_protenix_c96_e_native}/\result{dh_protenix_l48_e_native} to
\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_native}/\result{dh_protenix_l48_c_native}
on Confirm96-B/Length48. G+ also improves. OpenFold Native improves by
\result{a_c96_native_gain}/\result{a_l48_native_gain}, and G+ by
\result{a_c96_gplus_gain}/\result{a_l48_gplus_gain}.
Yet direct ESMC--ESM2 changes in $\Delta_F$ and $\Psi$ have intervals including
zero for both predictors. Protenix retains positive interaction intervals;
OpenFold establishes them with neither Train384 feature source. Higher
structural quality therefore need not coincide with an established change in
rotation sensitivity. The substitution changes PLM family, feature width,
cache precision and input-projection size together; it does not isolate model
size or test intermediate-layer or multilayer features.

\label{sec:limits_results}
In the matched Protenix Train384/ESM2 follow-up, Native
exceeds G+ by \result{pt384_c96_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_c96_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_c96_gdiffHi}] on Confirm96-B and
\result{pt384_l48_gdiff}
[\result{pt384_l48_gdiffLo}, \result{pt384_l48_gdiffHi}] on Length48.
The differences are positive for 78/96 and 43/48 targets and all three paired
seed means. G+ itself improves over query-only by \result{pt384_c96_gquery}
and \result{pt384_l48_gquery} in mean score. G+ is thus a functioning explicit-factor
comparator under the shared learning rate and budget, not an optimized performance bound.
For Protenix Train384/ESMC on Confirm96-B, the Native head gap is
$F-G^+=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_native_minus_gplus}$, while
$RF-RG^+=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_rotated_head_gap}$ after rotation.
Their difference is $\Psi=\result{dh_protenix_c96_c_pair_interaction}$.
Thus a substantial head gap remains under rotation. This mean identity is
not a causal percentage decomposition or an additional significance test.
The ESM2 long-chain comparison combines historical H100 predictions with new MI250
G+ predictions; replay checks are limited engineering evidence, not a
panel-wide numerical error bound (Appendix~\ref{app:engineering}).

OpenFold Train384 gives
\result{of384_c96_direction}
[\result{of384_c96_directionLo}, \result{of384_c96_directionHi}] on Confirm96-B,
without an established advantage. On Length48 it gives
\result{of384_l48_direction}
[\result{of384_l48_directionLo}, \result{of384_l48_directionHi}].
G+ exceeds the native factor head on the two OpenFold long-chain comparisons;
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:gplus} gives all paired competitiveness estimates. A direction benefit within
one constrained family can coexist with a better generic adapter.

AtlasFold establishes neither useful adaptation nor a direction advantage or
interaction with either feature source. Its high baseline and native AtlasLM
are not isolated explanations. Specified comparisons mix backends, including
Atlas ESMC's third seed and historical Protenix ESM2 Length48; these limitations
remain explicit in Appendix~\ref{app:dh_fourcells}.

Training-set changes provide another boundary. At fixed Full/1536, the Factor
direction effect changes by \result{pt_scale} in Protenix and
\result{of_scale_c96} in OpenFold on Confirm96-B; OpenFold Length48 establishes
no change. All four OpenFold/ESM2 mean scores improve: rotated Factor catches
up without an absolute Native decline. The direct, post-hoc change of the
complete interaction, $\Psi_{384}-\Psi_{96}$, is
\result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddt}
[\result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddtLo}, \result{psi_train_c96_ca_lddtHi}] on
Confirm96-B; Length48's interval includes zero (Appendix~\ref{app:training_psi}).
Data coverage, training-set composition and repetitions (16 versus 4 average
passes) change together, so neither difference identifies a universal data-size
or convergence effect.

\paragraph{A global-norm alternative.}
\label{sec:norm_cross}
A robustness analysis crosses learned residual source and global Frobenius
norm for fixed Protenix Train384/Full/1536 models: 24 observed Confirm96-B targets,
720 complete predictions on the same backend. Native-source advantages are \result{cross_ca_D_mN} at the
native norm and \result{cross_ca_D_mR} at the rotated norm; the symmetric
source effect is \result{cross_ca_Edir}
[\result{cross_ca_EdirLo}, \result{cross_ca_EdirHi}]. Thus global norm alone is
insufficient to explain this gap. Normalized residuals still differ in learned
content, local magnitudes and channel organization. This is not a pure
channel-rotation experiment (complete four cells in Appendix~\ref{app:norm_cross}).

\paragraph{Budget dependence remains unresolved.}
Saved 384/768/1536 checkpoints of both OpenFold training sizes were evaluated
on fixed Dev8 (Appendix~\ref{app:checkpoint_curves}). All four cells improve in
mean score from 384 to 1536, while $\Psi$ is nonmonotone and all six node
intervals include zero. This developmental diagnostic neither establishes
convergence nor a uniform rotation-induced learning delay; it does not select
a checkpoint or replace final Confirm96-B evaluation.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}
\paragraph{Predictors and information.}
We freeze Protenix-Mini/Tiny default checkpoints, official AF2
\texttt{model\_3\_ptm} weights through OpenFold, and AtlasFold with native
AtlasLM-3B retained. Evaluation inputs exclude homologous sequences,
templates and reference structures; adapter training uses structural
supervision. All four arms within each recipe use the same frozen ESM2-35M features
\citep{lin2023evolutionary} (480 channels, layer 12), or frozen ESMC-600M final-layer features
\citep{candido2026language} (1152 channels; Section~\ref{sec:esmc}).
Unadapted baselines lack these added features, so gains over baseline combine
feature access and adaptation. Within each head, Native--Rotated holds feature
access fixed and measures the effect of output rotation after separate training.
Table~\ref{tab:recipes} summarizes the paths.

\paragraph{Training and evaluation data.}
\label{sec:panels}
Train24, Train96 and Train384 are nested training sets containing 24, 96 and
384 chains, respectively; Dev8 is the development set. Train384 has no
target-ID overlap with Dev8 or either Confirm96 panel.
Confirm96-A supports the historical Factor--Generic comparison; Confirm96-B
supports the original Protenix direction comparison. Both contain 96 short-chain
targets selected from the existing Protein Data Bank (PDB) structure catalog
\citep{berman2000pdb}: single-model
X-ray entries released by 2021-09-30, resolution at most 2.5\,\AA, standard
amino acids and at least 90\% mapped C$\alpha$ coverage. Each Confirm96 panel
contains 24 targets in each of four length strata (128--191, 192--255,
256--319, 320--384). The release cutoff for newly selected candidates does
not apply to the inherited Train24/Dev8.
Table~\ref{tab:data_composition} distinguishes set construction
and first use. Length48 comprises 16 targets in each prespecified range
385--512, 513--640 and 641--768; actual lengths are 392--750, beyond the
Train384 adapter training range of 152--383. The original direction and
length studies selected their evaluation targets before scoring; later uses
of those panels are observed follow-ups.

Fresh96 and Fresh192 test fixed OpenFold Train96/ESM2 and Protenix
Train384/ESMC models, respectively, on newly selected short-chain targets.
Their four length strata contain 24 and 48 targets each, with realized lengths
of 129--384 and 128--383 residues. Selection precedes new predictions and
excludes documented prior project exposure; each study's sole primary
endpoint is its four-cell interaction $\Psi$
(Appendices~\ref{app:fresh96} and~\ref{app:fresh192}). Sequence screening and
study-specific exclusions are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:chronology}; they
do not establish family or foundation-model pretraining isolation.

\paragraph{Training and pairing.}
The original Protenix direction study trains on Train24 for 384 updates,
with Native--Rotated under the tangent construction as the prespecified primary
comparison and the corresponding Full comparison as secondary.
The larger-set studies use Full on Train96 and Train384, with 1536 updates
in each case. OpenFold uses Full on Train96 and Train384, each for 1536 updates;
AtlasFold trains on Train96 for 1536 updates.
Within each core four-cell recipe, heads share data order, three training seeds, random-condition
rules and optimizer settings. Each of three fixed rotations uses all three seeds;
G+ also has three native and nine rotated fits in each core four-cell setting.
The prediction and compensation studies use their separately specified rotation
sets and parameter groups.
We evaluate the fixed final checkpoint with one inference sample per full
sequence, without cropping. Evaluation labels are not used to select
checkpoints or inference samples. The predictor-specific task losses
backpropagate only through the final recycle in every arm of the core recipes.
The separate Protenix budget follow-up
continues all 24 models to 3072 updates; the OpenFold schedule follow-up
retrains 36 models with one rotation and gradients across four passes
(Appendices~\ref{app:budget_extension} and~\ref{app:schedule_retraining}).

\paragraph{Scores and interaction.}
Our primary score is C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT: for all fixed reference pairs within
15\,\AA, average distance-preservation credit at thresholds 0.5, 1, 2 and
4\,\AA, then give each target equal weight. Missing prediction pairs receive
zero credit without changing the reference denominator. This is a C$\alpha$
pair-averaged variant of lDDT \citep{mariani2013lddt}. Residue-average C$\alpha$
lDDT and fixed-correspondence TM-score \citep{zhang2004tm} are supplementary.
For the core three-seed, three-rotation comparisons, let $S_{H,i,s}$ denote
the Native score for head $H\in\{F,G+\}$ on target $i$ after training with
seed $s$; $S_{H,R_r,i,s}$ is its counterpart trained with rotation $R_r$.
For a panel of $N$ targets, define
\begin{equation}
 d_{H,i}=\frac13\sum_s\left(S_{H,i,s}-\frac13\sum_rS_{H,R_r,i,s}\right),
 \quad \Psi_i=d_{F,i}-d_{G+,i},\quad
 \Psi=\frac1N\sum_i\Psi_i.\label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
The single-rotation schedule study replaces the inner rotation average by R1.
We write $\Delta_H=N^{-1}\sum_i d_{H,i}$ for the signed Native--Rotated
score difference; positive values favor Native. Thus
$\Psi=\Delta_F-\Delta_{G+}$ compares signed score differences, not absolute rotation
sensitivity. Positive $\Psi$ alone implies neither $\Delta_F>0$ nor
$|\Delta_F|>|\Delta_{G+}|$ and does not rank absolute prediction quality.
\paragraph{Uncertainty and study identity.}
For structure-score contrasts, we resample complete target records for 20,000
paired bootstrap draws, preserving within-target seed/rotation pairing and
protocol-specific stratification. Fresh192 and its budget extension resample
within four fixed length strata. Intervals condition on the fitted models;
seeds, rotations and supplementary metrics are not additional independent
proteins. Reported confidence intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons.
Separately, the OpenFold feature-substitution study applies Holm correction
\citep{holm1979simple} to the $p$ values of its three prespecified secondary
tests (Appendix~\ref{app:esmc}). No joint correction is applied across all
follow-up studies.

Table~\ref{tab:study_roles} separates panel novelty from each four-cell study's
endpoint role. The output-map experiment predeclares rotated Factor's mean
gain and its additional gain over Native across two selected rotations;
its original execution and same-seed repeat are reported separately
(Appendix~\ref{app:e2_compensation}). The reconstruction prediction test uses
eight rotations as its units and an exact permutation test for its primary
association (Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}). Dev8 checkpoint diagnostics,
matched-G+ and norm-exchange comparisons retain their distinct questions
and endpoints in the corresponding appendices.

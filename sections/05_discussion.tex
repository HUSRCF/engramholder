\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The strongest evidence concerns a fixed Protenix/ESMC recipe: Factor's signed
Native--Rotated contrast exceeds G+'s on prospectively locked Fresh192 targets,
and the interaction remains positive after one prespecified budget doubling.
Factor's own penalty decreases with extra optimization. Together, these findings
show persistence across two tested budgets while allowing substantial learning
of rotated updates. They establish neither convergence nor a universal
parameterization law; OpenFold's Fresh96 primary interaction remains
unestablished.

The schedule study gives an evaluation consequence: First-only has the largest
interaction but the lowest quality, while All's positive interaction does not
establish a positive Factor contrast. Absolute scores and both signed row
contrasts are therefore needed to interpret $\Psi$. An adapter can be useful
without a large native-orientation advantage, and a large interaction does not
select a better adapter or schedule. This amended R1-only study changes training
and inference together and does not identify repeated writing or feedback as
the unique cause.

The explanatory interventions place further limits on the inference.
Shared compensation improves the two selected rotated Factor arms against
fixed no-$C$ controls in both executions, but its extra benefit over Native
was not re-established in the repeat. Output freedom and joint optimization
change together; the intervention identifies neither expressivity as the
unique cause nor a learned inverse. The failed reconstruction prediction
also leaves the trained penalty without an established predictor.
A separately retrained residual-anchor intervention did not establish the
predicted average benefit of live anchors to rotated Factor; the two selected
rotations responded in opposite directions (Appendix~\ref{app:anchor_intervention}).
It contains no $C$, so whether compensation depends on live anchors remains
untested. Norm exchange constrains a global-scale explanation while leaving
content and local magnitudes coupled. Across-head comparisons also change
nonlinearities and residue/pair organization, without proved function-class
inclusion. Dense, signed-permutation and mean-preserving controls have distinct
scopes; ideal coordinate compatibility does not establish FP32 trajectory identity.

The three kinds of follow-up answer different questions: new targets test
fixed fitted models, budget continuation evaluates those models after further
training, and the same-seed compensation repeat probes execution variability
without adding independent seeds or retrained no-$C$ controls. Target intervals
condition on fitted models and rotations; they do not quantify reliability over
new training runs. The compensation executions are not pooled, and differing
interval significance does not test a between-execution population difference.
The OpenFold feature study applies its prespecified Holm correction; no
correction is applied jointly across all follow-up studies.

ESMC improves quality without establishing changed signed contrasts; feature
family, width, precision and projection size vary together. AtlasFold adds no
established adaptation benefit. Dev8 curves do not establish convergence.
Reused panels, one inference sample, backend differences and incomplete
exposure records limit scope. Sequence screening guarantees neither family
nor foundation-model isolation. Cross-predictor comparisons change losses and
interfaces, and gaps to native single-sequence systems limit deployment claims.

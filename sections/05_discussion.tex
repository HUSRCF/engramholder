\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
Under the OpenFold Train96/ESM2-35M recipe, a head built through a fixed native
operator is more rotation-sensitive than a head whose free output layer can
absorb the transformation. The interaction identifies a parameterization-dependent
effect between two head architectures, but does not isolate the fixed decoder,
function-class constraints or optimization geometry. No inclusion of Factor's
function class within G+ has been proved. Nor does it imply that the factor
head is best: matched comparisons favor Native in Protenix and G+ in some
OpenFold settings. AtlasFold establishes no adaptation gain; its high baseline
has not been isolated as the cause.

The ESMC follow-up shows that substantially higher structural scores can occur
without an established head-by-rotation interaction. Train384/ESM2 also does not
establish that interaction, and direct ESMC--ESM2 changes in $\Delta_F$ and
$\Psi$ remain uncertain. These results cannot identify ESMC substitution as the
cause of the lack of an established interaction. The substitution changes PLM
family, feature width, cache precision and trainable input-projection size
together; their individual contributions to improved scores remain unknown.

The norm exchange limits an overall-scale explanation of one Protenix gap,
without separating learned content, local magnitudes and channel organization.
Local diagnostics retain their negative boundaries: oracle compatibility did
not establish a positive association with target-level folding gains, small equal-norm learned directions
showed no established native advantage, and bounded Train8-to-Dev8 updates
showed no established mean positive transfer. Their recipes, states, scales
and endpoints differ from complete inference; the origin of beneficial learned
residuals remains unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}).

Intervals describe target variation conditional on fitted models, not all
retraining or research-selection uncertainty. Convergence at the fixed
1536-update budget has not been established. Three seeds and rotations cover
limited instances; several studies reuse observed targets. One inference sample
per system does not estimate sampling variability. Sequence screening ensures
neither family independence nor exclusion from foundation-model pretraining,
and historical exposure remains incompletely characterized. Cross-predictor
comparisons change interfaces, representations and losses alongside the backbone.
The substantial gap to strong native single-sequence systems also limits
deployment claims. These findings delimit a conditional design factor, not a
universal adapter prescription.

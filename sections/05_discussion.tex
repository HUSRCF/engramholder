\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The OpenFold interaction is the clearest evidence that a rotation intervention
must be interpreted together with the adapter parameterization. Under a common
recipe, a head built through a fixed native operator is more sensitive than a
head whose free output layer can absorb the same transformation. This sharpens
the original direction observation while leaving two sources of sensitivity
entangled: the constraints on representable updates and the optimization geometry
of their parameters. Establishing their separate contributions would require a
different experiment, not a reinterpretation of the present contrast.

The structural results also distinguish three questions: whether adaptation
helps an unadapted system, whether native orientation helps within a constrained
family, and whether that family is preferable to a generic alternative. Protenix
supports direction and length-transfer effects and outperforms matched G+ at
Train384, while OpenFold illustrates that G+ can be more effective even when
direction matters. AtlasFold provides no
established adaptation gain in this setting. Its high baseline is descriptive;
we have not isolated a ceiling effect, nor identified an architectural component
that explains the difference between predictors.

The full-inference intervention directly tests an alternative explanation:
that the learned residual's global norm accounts for its structural advantage.
Native-source updates remain better at both actual norm donors. Thus the
structural difference depends on properties beyond overall scale, including
information retained in the normalized tensor. This identifies a limit of a
norm-only explanation; it does not separate learned content, local magnitude
patterns and channel organization, or locate a specific downstream computation.

Local diagnostics retain their narrower conclusions. Oracle compatibility did
not establish a positive association with target-level folding gains; learned
directions at a small common amplitude did not establish a native advantage.
The bounded Train8-to-Dev8 study also did not establish mean positive transfer.
These results coexist with the full-inference finding because the model recipe,
state, scale and endpoint differ. How training produces the beneficial residual
organization remains unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}).

Our intervals quantify target variation conditional on the fitted models, not
all uncertainty from retraining or research selection. One fixed inference sample
per system does not characterize sampling variability; paired RNG controls
comparison noise rather than estimate that variability. Three rotations and seeds
sample a limited family; several studies reuse observed panels. Sequence
screening does not ensure target-family independence, and cumulative historical
exposure and foundation pretraining overlap remain incompletely characterized.
Cross-predictor comparisons change interfaces, representations and training losses
along with the backbone. Finally, the absolute gap to strong native single-sequence
systems limits deployment claims. The evidence supports a conditional design
factor for frozen adaptation rather than a universal adapter prescription.

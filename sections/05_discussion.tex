\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
Under fixed training budgets, four-cell controls establish parameterization-dependent
sensitivity in specified Protenix and OpenFold recipes. For a recipe selected
on earlier data, Fresh192 provides prospective evidence of greater Factor
rotation sensitivity on new targets. Its inference concerns the fixed
Protenix/ESMC models and screened short-chain population; OpenFold's Fresh96
primary interaction remains unestablished. Shared compensation improves
rotated Factor in both executions. The rotated-arm gain has similar point
estimates, whereas Native gains more in the repeat, in which relative
mitigation was not re-established. This distinction separates an intervention's
useful quality gain from its extra benefit to rotated models.

The Protenix interaction remains positive after a fixed doubling of the update
budget; its paired change does not establish either a decline or invariance.
The OpenFold schedule study gives a complementary warning: First-only has
the largest interaction but the lowest quality, and All's positive interaction
does not establish a positive Factor direction effect. Row contrasts and
absolute scores are therefore necessary to interpret $\Psi$, rather than
treating it as an adapter-selection score. Schedule-dependent retraining does
not identify repeated writing or feedback as the unique cause.

Dense rotations, signed permutations and the mean-preserving rotations used for
prediction and compensation are distinct transformation families.
Signed-permutation compatibility with ideal AdamW does
not establish strict FP32 trajectory correspondence.
Channel compensation changes output freedom and joint optimization together;
it identifies neither
a learned inverse nor expressivity as the unique cause, and does not establish
elimination of $\Psi$. Across-head controls also change nonlinearities and
residue/pair organization, without proved function-class inclusion.

The prespecified teacher-update reconstruction score showed no established
positive association with post-training Native--Rotated score gaps across
the eight tested rotations
($\rho=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_rho}$, exact one-sided
$p=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_p}$; Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}). The compensation
intervention does not validate that ranking. A separately retrained
comparison with recycle-matched query-only references did not support the
predicted average benefit of live residual anchors to rotated Factor; the two
selected rotations responded in opposite directions
(Appendix~\ref{app:anchor_intervention}). This intervention contains no $C$,
leaving whether $C$'s benefit depends on live anchors untested.
Norm exchange constrains a global-scale explanation while leaving content
and local magnitudes coupled. Earlier local and shared-transfer diagnostics
retain their negative results in Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}.

ESMC improves quality without establishing changed direction sensitivity;
family, width, precision and projection size vary together. AtlasFold adds no
established adaptation benefit. Input complementarity and downstream use remain
candidate explanations, alongside finite-budget optimization limits.

Target intervals condition on fitted models and rotations; they do not estimate
robustness to retraining or transfer across recipes. The OpenFold feature study
applies its prespecified Holm correction; no correction is applied jointly
across all follow-up studies. The compensation executions reuse the same seeds and no-$C$
baselines and are not pooled; their differing interval significance does not
test a between-execution population difference.
Dev8 curves do not establish convergence. Reused panels, one inference sample,
backend differences and incomplete exposure records limit scope.
Sequence screening guarantees neither family nor foundation-model isolation.
Cross-predictor comparisons change losses and interfaces; gaps to native
single-sequence systems limit deployment claims. Evaluation should consider
output freedom and task training alongside local spectra under explicit budgets.

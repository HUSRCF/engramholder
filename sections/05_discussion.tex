\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The Protenix interaction persists at both tested budgets and across the
additional training and inference seeds. Its magnitude does not select
the best head or schedule. The MSA studies instead support testing adapters
under the intended input depth and selecting head and schedule by absolute
validation quality with comparable tuning budgets. Gains under artificial
MSA caps do not establish a universal depth threshold or a replacement
for full-MSA prediction.

The compensation, reconstruction, residual-anchor and new rotation
prediction tests do not validate a mechanism for the interaction
(Appendices~\ref{app:prediction_e1}, \ref{app:anchor_intervention}
and~\ref{app:rotation_prediction}). G+'s function-class closure does not
make AdamW rotation-equivariant; the heads also differ in computation and
parameter sharing. Equal update counts establish neither equal compute
nor convergence, and schedule retraining changes training and inference together.

On 24 observed Protenix targets, the native residual source retains an
advantage at matched global norms (Appendix~\ref{app:norm_cross}).
This source contrast includes learned content and relative pair/channel
magnitudes, so it does not isolate a causal effect of channel orientation.

Target intervals condition on fitted models; exploratory seed-resampling
intervals use only three new training seeds. The selected recipe and fixed
or restricted rotations limit generalization. There is no joint
multiplicity correction across follow-up studies; study-specific corrections
are reported separately.

AtlasFold showed no established adaptation benefit. The original Protenix
evaluation used one inference sample per target; the additional training
cohort used three paired inference seeds. Differing interfaces, losses and
backends restrict cross-predictor interpretation. CASP16 covers eight primary targets,
with no foundation-model or historical-MSA exposure isolation; it is not
a comprehensive CASP benchmark. Sequence screening does not establish
family independence. Higher full-MSA baseline scores limit deployment claims.

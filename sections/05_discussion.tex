\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The findings separate three questions. Trained four-cell controls establish
parameterization-dependent sensitivity in specified Protenix and OpenFold
recipes. Within Factor, shared orthogonal compensation provides a useful
average intervention. Neither finding supplies a reliable predictor of direction
cost or implies that Factor is the better head: Protenix favors Native over G+,
while some OpenFold comparisons favor G+. Protenix's observed-panel support
does not replace OpenFold's unestablished Fresh96 primary interaction.

These controls narrow different explanations. Dense rotations, signed
permutations and the mean-preserving E1/E2 family are distinct transformations.
Signed-permutation compatibility with ideal AdamW does not establish strict
FP32 trajectory correspondence. E2 tests Factor's direction cost, not elimination
of the original Factor/G+ interaction. Its output freedom and joint optimization
change together; the result identifies neither a learned inverse nor expressivity
as the unique cause. Across-head controls additionally change nonlinearities and
residue/pair organization; no function-class inclusion has been proved.

The eight-rotation teacher-reconstruction predictor failed
($\rho=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_rho}$, exact one-sided
$p=\result{e1_ca_lddt_x_p}$; Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}). E2 does not rescue
that ranking. Norm exchange limits a global-scale explanation without separating
learned content and local magnitudes. Local compatibility, small equal-norm
learned directions and bounded shared-transfer diagnostics retain their
negative boundaries (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}).

ESMC improves quality without establishing a change in direction sensitivity;
family, width, precision and projection size change together. AtlasFold adds no
established adaptation benefit. Input complementarity and downstream use remain
candidate explanations, not selection rules.

Intervals condition on fitted models and limited rotations, excluding full
retraining and research-selection uncertainty; follow-ups lack joint correction.
The Dev8 curves do not establish convergence. Reused panels, one inference sample,
specified backend differences and incomplete historical exposure limit scope.
Sequence screening guarantees neither family nor foundation-model isolation.
Cross-predictor comparisons also change losses and interfaces, and gaps to native
single-sequence systems limit deployment claims. These results motivate evaluating
output freedom and task training alongside local spectra, within explicit budgets.

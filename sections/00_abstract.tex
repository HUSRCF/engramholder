\begin{abstract}
Small adapters can alter the internal representations of a frozen protein
structure predictor, but their effectiveness may depend on both the form and
the orientation of the updates. We study this dependence using query-anchored
factor residuals composed through pretrained native operators. Fixed orthogonal
controls rotate residual channels, and every adapter is trained from zero
residual under paired conditions. Our central comparison adds a generic head
with explicit factor access and a free output layer that can absorb the rotation.
In an OpenFold follow-up on previously observed targets, rotation sensitivity
interacts with head parameterization: the difference-in-differences is
\result{inter_c96_interaction} in C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT on Confirm96-B
(95\% target-bootstrap interval
[\result{inter_c96_interactionLo}, \result{inter_c96_interactionHi}]).
Separately locked Protenix studies establish native-direction effects and an
advantage of \result{pt_length} on proteins beyond the adapters' training-length
range. The scope is conditional: an expanded-data OpenFold comparison does not
establish a direction advantage, generic heads can be more effective, and the
tested AtlasFold configuration establishes no native-direction benefit.
These results identify orientation as a consequential aspect of constrained
adaptation, without attributing the interaction solely to expressivity or
claiming universal superiority of native factor heads.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Small adapters modify a frozen protein structure predictor, but comparable
update forms need not yield comparable adaptation. We study query-anchored
factor residuals composed through pretrained native operators. Fixed orthogonal
controls rotate residual channels; all adapters are trained from the same
zero-residual function under paired conditions. A generic head with explicit
factor access and a free output layer provides a rotation-closed function-class
control. Under fixed 1536-update recipes, observed-panel follow-ups establish
head-by-rotation interactions in OpenFold Train96/ESM2-35M and Protenix
Train96/ESM2-35M: \result{inter_c96_interaction} and
\result{p96_pair_interaction} C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT, respectively.
The fixed OpenFold models give \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}]
on 96 prospectively locked targets, leaving that primary interaction
unestablished. Protenix Train384 retains direction effects and positive
follow-up interaction intervals with ESMC-600M; OpenFold Train384 and the tested
AtlasFold configurations do not establish an interaction. ESMC improves
Protenix and OpenFold structural scores without establishing a change in their
direction effects. Separately locked Protenix length transfer and matched-norm
checks extend the evidence without identifying a training mechanism. These
controls distinguish adaptation gains, rotation sensitivity and head
competitiveness: native orientation can matter, but neither guarantees useful
adaptation nor makes factor heads uniformly superior to generic alternatives.
\end{abstract}

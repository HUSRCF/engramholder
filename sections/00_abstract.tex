\begin{abstract}
Matching local spectra need not produce matching adaptation behavior in a
frozen predictor. We study residuals composed through protein predictors'
native pair-generating operators, using independently trained orthogonal
controls from a common zero-residual function. A generic head with explicit
factor access and a free output layer supplies a rotation-closed control.
Under fixed 1536-update recipes, observed-panel four-cell studies find greater
rotation sensitivity for Factor than G+ in OpenFold and Protenix Train96/ESM2,
with C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT interactions of \result{inter_c96_interaction} and
\result{p96_pair_interaction}. On 96 prospectively locked targets, the fixed
OpenFold models yield \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}];
the primary interaction is not established. A shared orthogonal compensator
provides useful average mitigation for two preselected OpenFold rotations,
whereas a reconstruction diagnostic fails to predict rotation costs.
Across settings, useful adaptation, rotation sensitivity and head competitiveness
remain distinct. These controls identify a conditional design effect: interface
evaluation must consider output freedom and task training alongside local
spectral properties, without treating direction sensitivity as method superiority.
\end{abstract}

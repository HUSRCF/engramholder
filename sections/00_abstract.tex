\begin{abstract}
Under a fixed task-training budget, does output-channel rotation affect
adaptation differently across parameterizations? We compare a structured head
(Factor), which composes residue-level increments through frozen protein
predictors' native pair-generating operators, with a generic head (G+) whose
free output layer makes its function class closed under rotation.
Native and rotated arms train separately from the same zero-residual function
under paired settings; orthogonal rotations preserve local writer spectra at
a common state. Our interaction $\Psi$ is the difference between their signed
Native--Rotated score contrasts, rather than absolute rotation sensitivity.
On 192 prospectively locked new targets, fixed Protenix/ESMC models establish
a C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT interaction of \result{p192_pair_interaction}
[\result{p192_pair_interactionLo}, \result{p192_pair_interactionHi}];
OpenFold's Fresh96 primary interaction remains unestablished.
In an observed-panel follow-up, the Protenix interaction remains positive
after a prespecified doubling of the update budget, while Factor's own
Native--Rotated gap significantly decreases.
An amended OpenFold schedule study on observed targets and one fixed rotation
finds the largest interaction at the lowest absolute quality.
Mechanism tests constrain the interpretation: shared orthogonal compensation
improves preselected rotated Factor arms, but its preferential benefit over
Native was not re-established in a same-seed repeat; a prespecified
reconstruction diagnostic did not establish the predicted ranking of
post-training rotation penalties.
Together, these findings motivate reporting absolute performance, signed
within-head contrasts and their interaction under explicit training budgets.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Matching local spectra does not guarantee matching adaptation behavior in a
frozen predictor. We study adapter residuals composed through protein
predictors' native pair-generating operators, with independently trained
orthogonal output controls that begin from the same zero-residual function.
We use a generic head with explicit factor access and a free output layer as
a control whose function class is closed under output rotation.
Under fixed 1536-update recipes, matched four-cell studies on observed
panels find greater rotation sensitivity for Factor than G+ in both
OpenFold and Protenix under Train96/ESM2, with C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT
interactions of \result{inter_c96_interaction} and
\result{p96_pair_interaction}. On 96 prospectively locked targets, however,
the same fixed OpenFold models yield an interaction of
\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}],
so the primary interaction is not established. On the observed panel, a
shared orthogonal compensator reduces mean direction cost by
\result{e2_pair_T_C}
[\result{e2_pair_T_CLo}, \result{e2_pair_T_CHi}] across two preselected
OpenFold rotations, whereas a reconstruction diagnostic fails to predict
rotation costs.
Across ested predictors and training settings, adaptation benefit, rotation
sensitivity, and head competitiveness remain distinct. These controls
identify a conditional design effect: structured interface evaluation must
consider output freedom and task training alongside local spectral
properties, without treating direction sensitivity as method superiority.
\end{abstract}

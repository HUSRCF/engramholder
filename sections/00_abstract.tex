\begin{abstract}
Small adapters modify the representations of a frozen protein structure
predictor, but comparable update forms need not yield comparable adaptation.
We study query-anchored factor residuals composed through pretrained native
operators. Fixed orthogonal controls rotate residual channels; all adapters
are trained from the same zero-residual function under paired conditions.
A generic head with explicit factor access and a free output layer provides
a control whose function class can absorb the rotation. In an OpenFold
follow-up on observed targets, rotation sensitivity interacts with head
parameterization: the difference-in-differences is
\result{inter_c96_interaction} in C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT on Confirm96-B
(95\% target-bootstrap interval
[\result{inter_c96_interactionLo}, \result{inter_c96_interactionHi}]).
Separately locked Protenix studies establish native-direction effects, including
an advantage of \result{pt_length} beyond the adapters' training-length range.
Full-inference interventions on 24 observed proteins retain a native-source
advantage at both matched residual norms, showing that global Frobenius norm
alone does not explain the observed gap under this intervention.
Effects depend on the predictor and training setting, and native factor heads
are not uniformly superior to generic alternatives. Together, these controls
identify orientation as a consequential aspect of constrained adaptation,
without uniquely attributing its effects to expressivity or training dynamics.
\end{abstract}

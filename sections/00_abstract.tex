\begin{abstract}
Does changing the orientation of an adapter update affect learning differently
across parameterizations? We study query-anchored factor residuals composed
through frozen protein structure predictors' native pair-generating operators.
Fixed orthogonal controls rotate residual channels, and each condition receives
full task training from the same zero-residual initial function. A generic head
with explicit factor access and a free output layer provides a rotation-closed
function-class control. Under fixed 1536-update recipes, observed-panel studies
find greater rotation sensitivity for Factor than G+ in OpenFold and Protenix
Train96/ESM2, with direct interactions of \result{inter_c96_interaction} and
\result{p96_pair_interaction} C$\alpha$ pair-lDDT. The fixed OpenFold models give
\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interaction}
[\result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionLo}, \result{fresh_ca_lddt_interactionHi}]
on 96 prospectively locked targets, leaving the new-target primary interaction
unestablished. Further settings retain both positive effects and clear boundaries:
head competitiveness and higher structural quality need not track rotation
sensitivity. The resulting evaluation distinguishes useful adaptation,
within-head direction effects and cross-head interactions. It establishes a
conditional role for orientation in these native interfaces, without identifying
a unique mechanism or a universal best adapter.
\end{abstract}

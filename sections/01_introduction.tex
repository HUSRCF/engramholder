\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Protein structure predictors map sequence information to geometry through
learned pairwise representations
\citep{jumper2021alphafold,gong2025protenix,seo2026atlasfold}.
We adapt a frozen predictor by adding residual updates to these
representations. Factor constructs these updates by composing predicted
residue-level factor increments through native pair-generating operators.
This construction inherits both a structured update form and an
output-channel orientation relative to the frozen downstream network.

An orthogonal output rotation preserves a residual's norm but can change
the frozen downstream network's response. We ask: \emph{under a fixed
task-training budget, does output-channel rotation affect adaptation
differently across parameterizations?}

Our four-cell design trains Native and Rotated versions of Factor and G+
separately from the same zero-residual predictor under paired settings.
G+ uses the same additional sequence features, receives interface anchors
explicitly, and has a free affine output layer. Each Rotated arm applies
a fixed orthogonal transform only to its completed residual. G+'s function
class is closed under output rotation, but its AdamW trajectories need not
match \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}. For each head $H$, we measure the signed
Native--Rotated score difference $\Delta_H$ and their interaction
$\Psi=\Delta_F-\Delta_{G+}$. Positive $\Psi$ means a larger signed difference
for Factor; it implies neither a positive Factor difference nor a larger
absolute response to rotation.

Fixed Protenix/ESMC models establish the primary interaction on 192 new
targets locked before prediction (Fresh192). Fixed OpenFold Train96 models
\citep{ahdritz2024openfold} do not establish their Fresh96 primary
interaction. Native Factor outperforms G+ in the tested Protenix recipes,
whereas G+ wins some OpenFold comparisons: the interaction does not determine
which head gives higher prediction quality.

On the now-observed Fresh192 panel, a prespecified doubling of the Protenix
update budget retains a positive interaction and reduces Factor's own score
difference, without establishing a paired change in $\Psi$. An amended
OpenFold schedule study on observed targets, using one fixed rotation R1,
finds the largest interaction with First-only injection, which gives the
lowest absolute quality. A larger $\Psi$ therefore need not identify a
better adapter or schedule.

We also test whether a shared trainable orthogonal output map benefits
rotated Factor more than Native Factor. Both selected rotated Factor arms
improve in the original execution and a same-seed retraining repeat.
Only the original establishes a larger compensation gain than Native's;
the repeat gives a smaller positive estimate of this gain difference whose
interval includes zero. A prespecified teacher-update reconstruction
diagnostic did not establish the predicted ranking of post-training
Native--Rotated score differences across eight tested rotations.

The four-cell design, new-target test and budget/schedule comparisons
support reporting absolute performance, signed within-head differences
and their interaction under explicit training budgets. The mechanism
studies delimit what this interaction identifies.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Protein structure predictors construct and refine pairwise representations
through learned computations that couple sequence information to geometry
\citep{jumper2021alphafold,gong2025protenix,seo2026atlasfold}.
Adapting a small interface while freezing the predictor makes it possible to
study how an update is used by an existing downstream computation. A natural
adapter reuses a pretrained pair-generating operator: it predicts residue-level
factor increments and combines them in the operator's native coordinates.
This construction inherits both a restricted form of update and an orientation
relative to the frozen downstream model. Their roles need not be the same.

Parameter-efficient adaptation can constrain weight updates, as in
LoRA \citep{hu2021lora}, or intervene on frozen-model representations, as in
ReFT \citep{wu2024reft}. Here we ask a more specific question about an internal
interface: \emph{does the effect of changing an update's output orientation
depend on how that update is parameterized?} A rotation can preserve local
spectral properties yet alter the representations received by a fixed
predictor. Observing a performance drop alone would leave open whether the
sensitivity concerns a particular constrained head or affects a more flexible
head to a similar degree.

We address this question with query-anchored residuals and trained rotation
controls. The adapters begin at the same unadapted function; orthogonal matrices
act only on residual pair channels, and each condition is trained independently
under paired settings. The factor head predicts increments that are composed
through a frozen native operator. A generic head, G+, receives the same extra
sequence features and explicit query factors, but has a free affine output
layer. An output rotation can be absorbed by reparameterizing this layer without
changing G+'s function class. This gives a discriminating control, although
coordinate-wise AdamW updates do not generally preserve an arbitrary orthogonal
parameter correspondence \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}.

The empirical evidence separates controlled effects from their scope. The original Protenix
comparisons and a separately locked length study establish native-direction
effects and their tested scope. A subsequent four-cell OpenFold experiment
\citep{ahdritz2024openfold} directly tests whether rotation sensitivity differs
between factor and generic parameterizations. This is an observed-panel
follow-up; the original Protenix and length studies retain their prospective
study identities. A matched Protenix Train384 comparison
also favors Native over G+, whereas OpenFold's generic head can outperform
Native. OpenFold does not establish the direction advantage in every setting,
and the tested non-OPM AtlasFold interface yields no established benefit.
The completed Train384 four-cell controls establish the interaction under
neither ESM2 nor ESMC, although the ESMC configuration improves OpenFold quality. A separate
full-inference norm exchange rules out overall residual magnitude as a sufficient
explanation of one Protenix gap; it does not isolate channel orientation from
the content learned by independently trained heads.

Together, these controls provide an evaluation strategy: matching parameter
counts, operator spectra and initial functions need not ensure comparable
adaptation behavior. They do not establish a rule for choosing the best head
for an unseen predictor.

Our contributions are an empirical parameterization-by-orientation interaction
under a matched training recipe; a concrete query-anchored residual construction
with explicit zero-initialization and local spectral controls; and an
assessment of transfer and limits across predictors, sequence feature sources,
training-set sizes, and adaptation lengths. A norm-exchange check tests an
alternative explanation of one learned-source gap. These results support the
following claim: \emph{native update
orientation can matter for constrained adaptation, but its effect depends on
the parameterization and frozen predictor; preserving native directions neither
guarantees adaptation gains nor makes native factor heads uniformly superior
to generic alternatives.}

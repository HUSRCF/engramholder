\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Protein structure predictors couple sequence information to geometry through
learned pairwise representations
\citep{jumper2021alphafold,gong2025protenix,seo2026atlasfold}.
An adapter can reuse a pretrained pair-generating operator, predicting
residue-level factor increments and combining them in its native coordinates.
Freezing the predictor lets us study two inherited properties of this
construction: its restricted update form and its orientation relative to
downstream computation.

Parameter-efficient methods constrain weight updates, as in LoRA
\citep{hu2021lora}, or intervene on representations, as in ReFT
\citep{wu2024reft}. We ask: \emph{after task training, does rotation cost depend
on the update parameterization, and can shared output freedom mitigate it?}
Matching parameter counts and local spectra does not specify how a frozen predictor uses an
update. Conversely, sensitivity to rotation need not imply superiority over
another adapter.

Our four-cell design compares native and rotated versions of two heads.
Factor composes query-anchored increments through a frozen native operator;
G+ receives the same extra sequence features and explicit factors, with a free
affine output layer. Orthogonal transforms act only on completed residuals.
Each arm receives independent task training under paired settings, beginning
at the same zero-residual function. G+'s output layer can absorb a rotation
without changing its function class, providing a control for the Factor effect.
This closure does not guarantee matching AdamW trajectories
\citep{loshchilov2019adamw}.

Fixed-budget studies find greater Factor rotation sensitivity in several
Protenix recipes and OpenFold Train96/ESM2 \citep{ahdritz2024openfold} on observed
panels. OpenFold Train384 and AtlasFold do not establish the interaction;
neither does the fixed OpenFold Train96 study's new-target primary test on
Fresh96. Native Factor outperforms G+ in Protenix, whereas G+ wins some OpenFold
comparisons. Thus direction effects, cross-head interactions and practical head
competitiveness answer different questions.

The controls have distinct scopes: within-head rotations preserve local writer
spectra at a common state; across heads, the initial function, approximate
parameter count and training recipe are matched, but Jacobians and effective
learning scales are not. The interaction therefore compares two complete
parameterizations, without establishing an expressivity hierarchy. A shared
orthogonal compensator provides useful average mitigation for two preselected
OpenFold rotations; separately, teacher-update reconstruction fails to predict
retraining costs.

This controlled empirical study contributes a native-operator residual,
trained four-cell comparisons, and a within-Factor compensation intervention.
The results separate useful adaptation, direction sensitivity and head
competitiveness. They motivate evaluating structured interfaces through output
freedom and task training alongside local spectra, without supplying a universal
adapter-selection rule.

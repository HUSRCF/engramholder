\subsection{Native heads with capped and full MSAs}
\label{app:followup_msa}

We compared Native Factor and Native G+ with their unadapted backbone
at matched MSA inputs on the previously evaluated Fresh192 (Protenix)
and Fresh96 (OpenFold) panels. For each input condition, we trained
each head with three seeds for 1,536 updates. The heads used matched
features and anchors: ESMC-600M on Train384 for Protenix Mini-default
and ESM2-35M on Train96 for OpenFold. The unadapted backbones did not
receive the added PLM features. These experiments compare absolute
scores and adaptation gains with MSA inputs; no output rotations were
applied.

Protenix Mini-default v0.5.0 retained its native MSA pathway. We
capped the assembled alignment at 32, 256 or 512 rows including the
query, before native recycle subsampling, or supplied the available
alignment subject to native assembly and sampling (full MSA).
Training and evaluation used the same MSA condition. Protenix evaluations used
four recycle passes, five diffusion steps and three paired inference
seeds with one structure per seed and target, in FP32 without templates.
The 32-row, 256-row and full conditions used the same runtime.
The 512-row experiment used separate hardware and a separate runtime;
its adapter comparisons use the baseline from that runtime.

OpenFold used \texttt{model\_3\_ptm} with up to 512 clustered and
1,024 extra MSA rows, four recycle passes, and no templates. The
adapter used the query row factors at the first main Evoformer
outer-product mean. Three fixed MSA feature-sampling seeds supplied
matched features to the two heads and the unadapted backbone.

\paragraph{Depth convention.}
The 32-row cap was fixed before evaluating the adaptation gains.
\href{https://elifesciences.org/articles/75751\#s4}{Del Alamo et al.\ (2022)}
used shallow MSAs for AlphaFold2 conformational sampling, reporting
\texttt{max\_extra\_msa} with \texttt{max\_msa\_clusters} set separately.
Their depth convention differs from our assembled row count.

The primary contrast was Factor minus the unadapted backbone at
full MSA in the Protenix 32/full and OpenFold studies, and at the
assigned cap in the separate Protenix 256- and 512-row experiments.
For each contrast we average over the applicable training seeds and
paired inference or feature-sampling seeds within target. The 95\%
intervals use 20,000 paired target-bootstrap draws conditional on the
fitted models and fixed seeds, within the four Fresh192 length
strata for Protenix and over the full panel for OpenFold.
Secondary intervals are unadjusted.

Factor outscored G+ and the matched unadapted Protenix backbone at
the 32- and 256-row caps (Table~\ref{tab:followup_msa_pair}). The
same-runtime difference in Factor gains between the 32-row cap and
full MSA, $(F_{32}-B_{32})-(F_{\mathrm{full}}-B_{\mathrm{full}})$,
was $\rebuttalMsaDepthGain$ in pair-lDDT. The unadapted full-MSA
backbone still scored higher in absolute terms than the capped models
in that comparison. Factor did not establish a gain at the 512-row
cap or with full Protenix MSA; with full OpenFold MSA, its pair-lDDT
was slightly below the baseline. The results motivate evaluating heads
at the intended MSA setting. Both training and evaluation depth changed
in the 32-versus-full comparison. Artificial caps do not establish a
universal depth threshold or transfer to naturally shallow alignments.

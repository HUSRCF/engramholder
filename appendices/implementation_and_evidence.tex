\section{Native operators and residual derivations}
\label{app:operators}
The default predictor maps 480-dimensional frozen ESM2 features through a shared
128-dimensional sequence encoder to residue-level increments. For Protenix and
OpenFold each of the two factor increments has width 32, and the pair residual
has 128 channels. The Protenix residual is computed by the bilinear expansion
in Eq.~\ref{eq:full}, avoiding subtraction of two large decoder outputs.
For an output weight reshaped as $W_{cuv}$, the three terms contract
$\delta a_{iu}W_{cuv}b_{jv}$, $a_{iu}W_{cuv}\delta b_{jv}$, and
$\delta a_{iu}W_{cuv}\delta b_{jv}$. The affine bias cancels in the difference.

The fixed Protenix scale originates from these 24 training-side teacher depths:
\begin{center}\small
114, 489, 490, 492, 493, 502, 502, 504, 504, 506, 507, 508,\\
509, 510, 511, 511, 512, 512, 513, 513, 513, 513, 513, 513.
\end{center}
Their median is $(508+509)/2=508.5$. It was not estimated from test proteins
or reselected for Train96/384. This historical MSA metadata is disclosed even
though task-only prediction does not take homologs as input. The residual's
$D/(D+10^{-3})$ multiplier never replaces the depth-one query baseline.

OpenFold recomputes its first-OPM anchors after pair-biased MSA updates on
each pass, so they can depend on previous adapted states. The residual
retains that OPM's mask and effective-row denominator; later blocks are
unchanged. AtlasFold uses its first LM difference/product update, with
factor width 128 and the native projection order. Its anchor depends on the
current pass's MLM mask, before fusion with recycled states, and does not
directly read adapted history. Native AtlasLM-3B is retained. Factor increments
depend on complete sequence features and shared predictor parameters, not on
the anchor or pass index. Neither interface inherits Protenix's depth constant.

G+ concatenates $[z_i,z_j,a_i,b_j,\operatorname{clip}(i-j,-32,32)/32]$ and
uses a Linear--GELU--Linear pair head. The final output layer has no activation
or extra output constraint. Its zero weight and bias recover the baseline.
For Protenix/OpenFold its hidden pair width is 192. AtlasFold's Factor/G+
parameter counts are 423,168/415,008, versus 373,824/378,144 in the OPM studies.
These are approximate size controls, not exact parameter matches.

For identical inputs, anchors and writer parameters, changing only the
output rotation gives $(RJ)^\top(RJ)=J^\top J$ for the local writer Jacobian.
A single-pair Jacobian with respect to free factor coordinates is distinct
from the Jacobian with respect to shared predictor parameters, which couples
residues and targets. Full single-pair rank would not establish rotation
closure of the shared update family; a smaller local rank would not establish
a native task advantage. This local identity neither compares independently
trained writers nor asserts spectral equivalence of the full recycled model.
Likewise, a nonzero injected residual, a change between predicted structures,
and improved accuracy against a reference structure are distinct observations.
For G+, the free final layer proves Eq.~\ref{eq:absorb}. Ordinary Euclidean
updates admit an orthogonal parameter correspondence under matched conditions;
AdamW's coordinate-wise moment accumulation generally does not commute with a
general rotation. Equality of G+'s representable classes therefore supplies a
control, not a prediction of identical training.

\section{Per-predictor implementation and training recipes}
\label{app:recipes}
The Protenix task-only direction recipe uses AdamW with learning rate
$5\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, betas $(0.9,0.999)$,
$\epsilon=10^{-8}$ and gradient clipping at 1. The original Mini direction
matrix includes Full and tangent at Train24/384 updates. Larger-set direction
comparisons use Full at 1536 updates. Frozen ESM2-35M representations, the
folding checkpoint, native decoder and query-feature contracts are preserved.
The loss is $4\mathcal L_{\rm MSE}+4\mathcal L_{\rm bond}+
4\mathcal L_{\rm smooth-lDDT}+0.03\mathcal L_{\rm distogram}$;
the existing protein-only bond mask is empty and confidence is excluded.
Only the last of four recycle passes retains the task gradient.

OpenFold loads official AF2 \texttt{model\_3\_ptm} weights and preserves all
48 main Evoformer blocks. The first block's OPM is adapted on each pass.
The selected common learning rate is $10^{-4}$, with AdamW weight decay
0.01 and global clipping at 1. The loss is FAPE plus 0.3 distogram plus
supervised-chi; confidence and masked-MSA auxiliary losses are excluded.
Three recycling iterations plus the initial pass give four trunk passes,
with task backpropagation through the last. The added rotated-G+ study reuses
this Train96/ESM2 recipe, three training seeds, data order and final step without new
learning-rate selection. That follow-up's primary endpoint is the Confirm96-B interaction.

AtlasFold uses the 260703 structure checkpoint and native AtlasLM-3B,
adding ESM2-35M only as adapter input. A bounded development calibration
selects one common $10^{-4}$ learning rate using mean Dev8 performance pooled
equally across Native, a fixed rotation, and G+; formal fits use an independent
set of training seeds. Training uses four trunk calls, BF16 autocast with FP32
native diffusion/loss, activation checkpointing, and no stochastic dropout.
The loss weights are 0.4 distogram and 2 diffusion (weighted MSE plus smooth
lDDT), with confidence excluded. Inference's recycle argument of four produces
five trunk calls; one sample uses the pinned native length rule of 20 diffusion
steps through 512 residues and 30 through 1024.

The common formal training seeds are 20260923/24/25 and ordinary rotation seeds
20261001/02/03. The preserved-mean Protenix controls use their separately locked
rotation family. Protenix prediction uses seed 101, four recycles, five steps,
FP32 torch kernels, TF32 and MC dropout off. OpenFold uses seed 20260921 and
four trunk passes without early stopping; AtlasFold uses seed 1. Different
backbones use native losses and inference budgets: equal update counts do not
mean equal computation. Frozen-parameter hashes, zero-initialization replay,
output-head gradients and subsequent encoder gradients are checked.

\section{Scoring, failure handling, and statistical analysis}
\label{app:scoring}
Reference C$\alpha$ positions and mappings are fixed across systems. Define
$\Omega_i=\{(a,b):a<b,\ 0<d^{\rm ref}_{ab}<15\text{\,\AA}\}$ and
$T=\{0.5,1,2,4\}$\,\AA. The primary per-target score is
\begin{equation}
 S_i=\frac{1}{|\Omega_i|}\sum_{(a,b)\in\Omega_i}
 \frac14\sum_{\tau\in T}\mathbf 1\{a,b\text{ predicted and finite}\}
 \mathbf 1\{|d^{\rm pred}_{ab}-d^{\rm ref}_{ab}|<\tau\}.
\end{equation}
Prediction failure or missing coordinates do not remove reference pairs from
the denominator. Global pair averaging and residue averaging can differ because
residues have different numbers of reference neighbors. We report both, and use
the true fixed-correspondence TM-score tool for supplementary structure scoring;
the earlier Kabsch-based proxy is not substituted for that metric.

A target's complete seed/rotation record is the paired resampling unit.
The original Protenix direction bootstrap uses seed 20260926, the original
Length48 bootstrap uses 20260928, and the OpenFold four-cell study uses 20260921,
each with 20,000 draws. The matched Protenix G+ study uses seed 20260926 and
20,000 draws on each panel. The paper generation script reads locked intervals
and independently reconstructs the six new G+ contrasts with the same rule;
it does not select a new resampling procedure. Original primary gate definitions
remain in the historical records; later comparisons do not replace them.
Unadjusted secondary intervals and limited training-seed coverage should not be
interpreted as a universal simultaneous confidence guarantee.

\section{Local compatibility and shared-transfer diagnostics}
\label{app:mechanisms}
At a shared query-only state, let $A_x$ map free factor increments to the
first-order update and let $g_x$ be the native task-loss gradient with respect
to that update. The diagnostic uses
$P_\lambda=A_x(A_x^\top A_x+\lambda I)^{-1}A_x^\top$ and separately records
$g_x^\top P_\lambda g_x/\|g_x\|^2$ and
$\|P_\lambda g_x\|^2/\|g_x\|^2$. Ridge shrinkage is not an exact orthogonal
projection. Equal-norm negative projected-gradient interventions verify local
task response with the preceding recycle states fixed. Their first-order
response is related by construction to the alignment quantities, so they are
not independent mechanisms.

The observed-panel local advantage did not establish a positive association
with the previously measured per-target folding gains. This oracle chooses
increments using each target's structure supervision, bypassing the shared
sequence predictor's learning problem. A later parameter-space differential
study passed independent writer and gradient-chain checks but encountered
responses near downstream numerical resolution; its original S0 remained
unpassed. A separately declared finite-step Train8-to-Dev8 study produced
measurable responses but did not establish mean positive transfer or a native
advantage. These distinct outcomes are retained rather than merged into one
successful mechanism claim.

\section{Engineering audits and recovery records}
\label{app:engineering}
Zero-residual replay, input/parameter gradients, frozen hashes, pairing, and
complete-sequence outputs are audited independently of quality-based model
selection. Backend reruns are numerical checks, not new biological samples.
In the AtlasFold final evaluation, broken cross-machine absolute symlinks caused
720 recorded engineering failures across the first five runs. Repairs retained
the same final checkpoints, reused the available short-chain CIFs, and generated
missing outputs under independent recovery reports. The final 2160 predictions
were complete; the original attempts were not erased or selected by quality.
Post-start supplementary checks in the original length study retain their
actual chronology rather than being relabeled as prelaunch checks.

For matched Protenix G+, three two-step prefixes passed before formal
continuation; those steps count within each completed 1536-update run.
All 432 formal predictions succeeded without HIP retries. Audits checked the
complete training order, paired noise levels, frozen-parameter and artifact
hashes; score contrasts were independently reconstructed. Confirm96-B uses
MI250 for both sides. Length48 reuses historical H100 Native/Rotated predictions
and new MI250 G+ predictions. Short-chain replay was exact, while a 750-residue
replay had relative coordinate L2 error 0.00032018, below the predeclared
$10^{-3}$ engineering tolerance. This limited replay does not establish a
numerical error bound for the entire long-chain panel.

\section{Signed-permutation control under the fixed AdamW recipe}
\label{app:signed_control}
This observed-panel OpenFold/Train96/ESM2/Full/1536 follow-up trains three
signed transforms across three seeds in Factor and G+: 18 fits and 2,592
successful predictions. Reusing six audited Native/G+ fits and query outputs
gives 3,600 records. Confirm96-B pair-lDDT $\Psi$ is primary; Length48 and other
metrics are secondary and unadjusted, not new-target confirmation.

Each transform is $(Tx)_j=s_jx_{\pi(j)}$ with $s_j\in\{-1,+1\}$ and fixed
permutation $\pi$. The three matrices have 63, 63 and 55 negative signs,
respectively, so $T\mathbf1\ne\mathbf1$: they are not pure permutations or
mean-preserving controls. The transform is applied to the complete residual
by gather-and-sign, leaving the baseline unchanged. Historical dense Factor
rotations were fused into the decoder; numerical implementations are not
claimed identical across these transformation families.

For G+'s free affine output, $W_T=T^\top W$ and $b_T=T^\top b$ yield the same
function. In ideal arithmetic, a signed coordinate permutation also maps
AdamW's first moment with signs and permutation, and its second moment by the
unsigned permutation. With matched random conditions, parameter groups, scalar
$\epsilon$, isotropic decay and global L2 clipping, this coordinate mapping is
compatible with the update. It differs from arbitrary dense rotations.
Small-head VJP and optimizer checks passed, but a strict correspondence of
complete FP32 training trajectories was not established. Non-significant G+
differences are not equivalence tests, nor do they rule out optimization or

\section{Shared orthogonal channel compensation within Factor}
\label{app:e2_compensation}
The separately locked compensation study (internally E2) uses the same
OpenFold/Train96/ESM2/Full/1536 recipe as the prediction study in
Appendix~\ref{app:prediction_e1}. It adds a shared channel map to Native and to
r20270107/r20270104, selected as low/high reconstruction error before the new
rotations' task training. Selection was not based on their later structure
scores. There are three paired seeds per condition: nine fits and 864 new
Confirm96-B predictions, all complete without prediction failures.
The original Native and rotated models provide the paired no-map controls.

\paragraph{A targeted output freedom.}
For the all-ones unit vector $u\in\mathbb R^{128}$ and orthonormal complement
$B\in\mathbb R^{128\times127}$, we use an exponential-map parameterization
\citep{lezcano2019orthogonal} on that complement:
\begin{equation}
 C=uu^\top+B\exp(A)B^\top,\qquad A^\top=-A.
\end{equation}
Its 8,001 free upper-triangular entries parameterize the full skew-symmetric
matrix. One $C$ is shared across targets, pairs and recycling calls within each
trained model. Native injects $C\Delta U_\theta$; Rotated injects
$CR\Delta U_\theta$. The baseline is unchanged. For a fixed residual, $C$
preserves its pairwise Euclidean norm and channel mean in ideal arithmetic.
Joint training can still change $\Delta U_\theta$ and its amplitude; the norm
of baseline plus residual is not controlled by this identity.

The rotation family belongs to
$\mathcal H=\{C\in SO(128):Cu=u\}\cong SO(127)$ in ideal arithmetic.
The unrestricted skew exponential covers this group: real orthogonal block
diagonalization gives planar rotations, each with a real skew logarithm.
For any $(\theta,C_N)$, choose $C_R=C_NR^\top\in\mathcal H$. Then
\begin{equation}
 C_RR\Delta U_\theta=C_N\Delta U_\theta,
 \qquad \mathcal F_{F+C}^{R}=\mathcal F_{F+C}^{I}.
\end{equation}
This is a function-class equality for the stated subgroup, not for arbitrary
$O(128)$ transforms. With paired initial states and random conditions, equal
injections imply equal frozen downstream states and hence equal subsequent
live anchors, extending the argument through recycling. No per-target or
per-round freedom is added to $C$.

Actual code uses $C=I+B[\exp(A)-I]B^\top$ for an exact identity at zero $A$;
finite-precision bases and rotations only approximate the ideal group.
Every model starts at $A=0$, $C=I$ and zero residual, rather than an inverse-$R$
hot start. The channel parameters receive zero gradient at that zero-residual
point, then learn jointly with the original head. They use AdamW at $10^{-3}$
with no decay; head parameters retain $10^{-4}$ and decay 0.01; all use one
global clipping norm of 1. Function-class equality therefore does not imply
mapped initial parameters, equivalent optimizer trajectories or equal final
performance. Orthogonal adaptation itself is not new
\citep{qiu2023oft,liu2023boft}; here it is a controlled intervention on rotation cost.

\paragraph{Paired endpoints and their identity.}
Define
\begin{equation}
 B_N=S(N+C)-S(N),\quad B_R^{C}=S(R+C)-S(R),\quad
 T_C=(N-R)-(N_C-R_C)=B_R^{C}-B_N,
\end{equation}
where the last expression uses scores, with the same target and seed paired
in each term. The original sealed design explicitly defines equal-weight averages of
$B_R^{C}$ and $T_C$ and the prediction that both are positive, with both rotations
and Native's own change also reported. The sealed analysis code implements
these averages before outcomes. These are prespecified mechanism contrasts,
not a new-target confirmation or a newly declared sole-primary endpoint.
The 20,000 synchronous whole-target draws (seed 20260924) retain the shared
Native control; duplicated uses of it are not independent observations.
Intervals condition on these fitted models and selected rotations and are
not jointly multiplicity-adjusted. Confirm96-B was already observed.

In the original execution, the three pair-lDDT seed marginals of average $T_C$ are positive, as are the
six seed-by-rotation marginals. Only r20270107 independently establishes a
positive $T_C$ interval; both rotated arms improve in absolute score. Average
residue-lDDT mitigation is supported, while average TM-score mitigation is not.
This supplies a useful average intervention in the tested setting, without
establishing equivalence, elimination for every rotation, a learned $R^{-1}$,
or a predictive rule for unseen settings. It does not validate the failed
reconstruction predictor. Changes in output freedom, parameterization and
joint optimization remain coupled.

\section{Residual-anchor intervention}
\label{app:anchor_intervention}
This study tests a specific feedback hypothesis: whether constructing the
residual from the adapted trajectory's live factors helps rotated Factor and
reduces its direction cost. It is internally called E3 in the prospective
series, distinct from the earlier diagnostic E3 in
Appendix~\ref{app:mechanisms}. It contains no channel compensator $C$.

\paragraph{Intervention and paired training.}
Let $L$ denote live residual anchors and $Q$ the query-only references for
the same sequence and recycle index $t$. At the first OPM of the intact
48-block Evoformer, the two paths are
\begin{align}
 U_{L,t}&=U_{0,t}^{\mathrm{live}}+\mathcal R
 \bigl[G(a_t+\delta a,b_t+\delta b)-G(a_t,b_t)\bigr],\\
 U_{Q,t}&=U_{0,t}^{\mathrm{live}}+\mathcal R
 \bigl[G(a^Q_t+\delta a,b^Q_t+\delta b)-G(a^Q_t,b^Q_t)\bigr],
\end{align}
where $\mathcal R$ is identity for Native and the fixed channel rotation for
Rotated. Each adapted path retains its own live OPM baseline, pair state,
four-pass recycling and structure module. The four reference pairs
$(a^Q_t,b^Q_t)$ come from four separate query-only passes, not a repeated
first-pass anchor. Mask, normalization and frozen decoder are unchanged.
One sequence-conditioned writer supplies the same factor increments at each
pass; only the anchors differ. This replaces neither the live baseline nor
the full recycling process, and does not make the update a cached Protenix
residual.

The recipe is OpenFold model\_3\_ptm, Train96, ESM2-35M layer 12 and Full,
with 1,536 updates and the original AdamW recipe. Native and the two rotations
r20270107/r20270104 are independently retrained under $Q$, each with the
three paired seeds. These rotations were selected and locked before their
task scores in the prediction study. All nine new fits start from the
original zero-output initialization, without a warm start or $C$.
The accepted $L$ models are reused from the prediction study. The 864 new predictions and
960 original query/Native/Rotated predictions yield 1,824 score records on
the already observed Confirm96-B panel; no target or rotation is replaced.

\paragraph{Predeclared quantities and statistical identity.}
Using scores with matched target and training seed, define
\begin{equation}
 T_A=(N_Q-R_Q)-(N_L-R_L),\qquad
 B_R^{\mathrm{anchor}}=R_L-R_Q.
\end{equation}
Both are averaged equally over the two selected rotations. The hypothesis
requires positive $T_A$ and positive $B_R^{\mathrm{anchor}}$, supported by
their intervals. The latter differs from compensation's
$B_R^{C}=S(R+C)-S(R)$; only display notation changes, not sealed field names.
The paired identity
\begin{equation}
 T_A=B_R^{\mathrm{anchor}}+(N_Q-N_L)
\end{equation}
holds for each target and seed. Thus a reduced gap can reflect a changed
Native reference as well as a changed rotated arm.

Pair-lDDT is primary; residue-lDDT and TM-score are supplementary measurements
from the same structures. This is an observed-panel, Factor-only intervention,
